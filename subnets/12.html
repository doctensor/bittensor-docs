What it is

ComputeHorde offers validators of other Bittensor subnets cheap, trusted GPU capacity: miners register executors, validators send them synthetic and organic jobs and check results on a trusted GPU. The chat names no customer subnet, and the live burn setting sends about 99% of miner weight to a team-chosen burn list.

How it works

Miners

Miners run executors (several GPUs behind one miner hotkey) that take jobs from validators. Only the A6000 class is documented as supported, with A100 next. They can post collateral with validators for organic jobs and run an optional DDoS shield.

Validators

Validators run synthetic jobs and relay organic jobs from a facilitator, checking results on a separate trusted GPU. Each block mints compute allowance per miner and validator pair, scaled by validator stake; a job spends allowance equal to its runtime. Validators then apply the burn described below before setting weights.

Incentive

Paid runtime per miner is summed by coldkey and split across that coldkey’s hotkeys. Validators send a share of weight to burn hotkeys; the share and list come from a config in the team’s public repository, so they change without a release. It read burn rate 0.9935 on 2026-11-05, unchanged since 2025-11-18, and the chain agrees: owner uids hold 99.37% of incentive. The team says it avoids drawing in miners before demand exists.

Roles and requirements

Miner High barrier

Runs executors with GPUs that take jobs from validators; pay is nearly all burned.

  • Non-owner uids hold about 0.6% of mechanism 0 incentive; the miner side is burned (chain, 2026-11-07).
  • The chain has 2 mechanisms; mechanism 1 gets 0% of emission and nobody sets weights on it (chain, 2026-11-07); its purpose is undocumented.

Validator High barrier

Sends synthetic and organic jobs, checks results on a trusted GPU and sets weights.

  • 10 permits held and 8 sets of weights; 3 permit holders have not set weights in over 5 tempos (2026-11-07).
  • Outside validators must follow the team's burn config to match the others.

Who holds the stake

Read 2026-11-07; a coldkey is not proof of one person.

  • The owner’s coldkey holds 71.2% of the alpha the read reached (1.47 M of 2.06 M, 96.1% of the staked total); counting the unread 3.9% either way, its share is between 68.4% and 72.3%.
  • One hotkey holds 70.0% and has a validator permit; the 10 permit hotkeys hold 76.5%, and owner-linked uids receive 61.6% of dividends.
  • 22 coldkeys hold 90% together; the next largest coldkey holds 3.1%. A chat claim of 2026-10-24 (owner near two thirds) fits that range.
  • Alpha transfers are switched off for this subnet on chain since at least 2025-10-01 (SN12 and SN29 are the only two); chat says holders have to unstake instead.

Bull case

  • Net outflow is about 1.1% of the pool in 30 days; the latest day was 3 TAO.

Bear case

  • Miner side burned: about 0.6% of incentive reaches outside miners.
  • No customer subnet or traffic named; owner called it barely alive (2026-07-03).
  • Owner-linked uids hold most emission, and the owner’s coldkey about 71% of staked alpha.

What would change this view: A lower burn rate, or a named subnet validating on ComputeHorde, would point up; outflow or a quiet repo would point down.

Getting started

  1. Read the live burn settings in the dynamic config repository first: about 0.6% of incentive reaches outside miners.
  2. Read the README and the miner and validator READMEs; the install scripts are in the repo root.
  3. Test a miner on testnet netuid 174 before registering on mainnet 12.
  4. A6000 is the only documented hardware class.

Caveats

  • The burn is team-set and can change without a release.
  • No traffic, revenue or customer subnet could be confirmed; no public repository shows another subnet validating on ComputeHorde.
  • Chat claims about the owner’s alpha sales and earnings were not checked and are not repeated.
  • What the alpha-transfer switch blocks was not tested; the validator and miner parts of the 2026-10-22 security commit were not audited.
