What it is

ComputeHorde aims to give validators of other Bittensor subnets cheap, trusted GPU capacity. Miners register executors, validators send them synthetic and organic jobs and check results with a trusted GPU. The chat names no customer subnet, and the live burn setting sends about 99% of miner weight to a team-chosen burn list.

How it works

Miners

Miners run executors (several GPUs behind one miner hotkey) that accept jobs from validators. Only the A6000 class is documented as supported, with A100 next. They can post collateral with validators to get organic jobs and run an optional DDoS shield.

Validators

Validators run synthetic jobs and relay organic jobs from a facilitator, checking results against a separate trusted GPU. Each block mints compute allowance per miner and validator pair, scaled by validator stake, and a job spends allowance equal to its runtime. Validators then apply the burn described below before setting weights.

Incentive

Paid runtime per miner is summed by coldkey and split across that coldkey’s hotkeys. Validators send a share of weight to a list of burn hotkeys; the share and list come from a config in the team’s public repository, so they change without a release. On 2026-10-30 it read burn rate 0.9935 and main-burner share 0.975, unchanged since 2025-11-18. The chain agrees: owner uids hold 99.37% of incentive and the top uid 96.9%; it rotates between cycles. The team’s stated reason is to avoid drawing in miners before demand exists.

Roles and requirements

Miner High barrier

Runs executors with GPUs that take jobs from validators; pay is nearly all burned.

  • Non-owner uids hold about 0.6% of mechanism 0 incentive; the miner side is burned (chain, 2026-10-30).
  • The chain has 2 mechanisms; only mechanism 0 was read, and the second one’s purpose is not documented.

Validator High barrier

Sends synthetic and organic jobs, checks results on a trusted GPU and sets weights.

  • 10 permits held and 8 sets of weights; 3 permit holders have not set weights in over 5 tempos (2026-10-30).
  • Outside validators must follow the team's burn config to match the weights of the others.

Bull case

  • Price and flow flat for 30 days; the repo shipped 5 code commits and library and SDK bumps.
  • Owner says the burn is deliberate and could be lowered, and integrations are in progress.

Bear case

  • Miner side burned: about 0.6% of incentive reaches outside miners.
  • No customer subnet or traffic named; owner called it barely alive.
  • Owner-linked uids hold most emission.

What would change this view: A lower burn rate, or a named subnet validating on ComputeHorde, would point up; outflow or a quiet repo would point down.

Getting started

  1. Read the live burn settings in the dynamic config repository first: about 0.6% of incentive reaches miners outside the owner’s uids.
  2. Read the README and the miner and validator READMEs; install_miner.sh and install_validator.sh are in the repo root.
  3. Test a miner on testnet netuid 174 before registering on mainnet 12.
  4. A6000 is the only documented hardware class; other GPUs do not count as that class.

Caveats

  • The burn is team-set and dynamic: validators follow a config repository, so the figures can change without a release.
  • No traffic, revenue or customer subnet could be confirmed; no public repository shows another subnet validating on ComputeHorde.
  • Chat includes accusations about the owner’s holdings; they were not checked on chain and are not repeated as fact.
  • Alpha transfers being disabled comes from chat and was not checked on chain; the 2026-10-22 security commit was not audited.
