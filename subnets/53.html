What it is

engy is an inference provider built on subnet 53. Buyers call one OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible API with prepaid billing, and GPU miners serve the requests for open models such as Kimi K3, GLM and DeepSeek. Miners are paid in proportion to the paid tokens they serve, subject to quality gates. The team says each response carries a fingerprint proving the named model produced it, and the team's own master validator computes and signs the weights that other validators copy.

How it works

Miners

Miners bring GPU boxes online and the gateway routes buyer requests to them. The current route is permissionless auto-provisioning, and on 2026-10-22 the team said the original onboarding process is retired: claim a gateway key with a hotkey, boot the team's golden serve image with that key, declare the model and SSH port in the provider dashboard, and the team verifies the box over SSH (hardware attestation on confidential-computing hosts, an SSH identity check otherwise) and activates it. The team keeps a management SSH channel into the image and says miners must leave the box alone; one miner objected on 10-22 that this is intrusive and rules out container hosts without nested Docker, and no alternative had been offered by 10-23. The repo still holds an older self-hosted miner for sglang, which the team called legacy on 2026-10-14. Models are downloaded after boot; for glm-5.3-flash the team said at least eight H100-class GPUs are needed. Miners reported qwen3.8-27b and glm-5.3-flash workers stuck in pending through 10-22.

Validators

The master validator is the team's own, and it holds the canonical checkpoints and does the verification. It samples requests, checks the activation fingerprints against the real model, scores every miner and model pair from the gateway request log, and publishes one signed result per epoch. Third-party light validators run on CPU only, fetch that result from the team's API, verify the signature against the pinned master hotkey and submit the same weights on chain. On chain (block 9343310, 2026-10-23) 15 uids hold validator permits and 13 had set weights recently; all 13 put their largest weight on the same miner, and none gives weight to uid 0. The master hotkey is the subnet owner hotkey and is registered as one uid that is the only one under the owner coldkey. Verification is sampled, and the team conceded on 2026-09-18 that its public materials describe it inconsistently.

Incentive

The team's docs say a miner's score for a model is the sum of prompt and completion tokens over paid, successful requests times a per-model rate that the team sets, and it is zero if a gate fails (acceptance, first-token and per-token latency, cheat verdicts). Scores are normalized into one weight vector, and with no billed traffic everything goes to the owner hotkey. Epochs are 7 days, and work in one epoch is applied on chain in the next. In the signed result for epoch 18 (10-14 to 10-21), 36 hotkeys received weight, the top hotkey about 11 percent and the top three about 32 percent; Kimi K3 made up about 56 percent of the scored value and glm-5.3-flash about 18 percent. No miner failed a gate and no audit verdicts were recorded. The published formula did not reproduce the signed scores from the public token counts, so treat it as a description of intent. On chain the incentive shares match that signed vector to within 0.01 percentage point, and the emission split is 18 percent to the owner, 41 percent to validators and 41 percent to miners, with nothing burned or recycled by that split. The team's buyback page lists burns of subnet alpha funded by revenue, 20,653 alpha in total, the latest on 10-17; those burns were not checked on chain.

Getting started

  1. Check the hardware: the team said at least eight H100-class GPUs for glm-5.3-flash; other models have their own needs and the full specs were still unpublished on 2026-10-14.
  2. Log in to provider.engy.ai with the hotkey and claim a gateway key.
  3. Boot the golden serve image (ghcr.io/hanlinai/engy-golden-serve:prod) with only that key in its environment; the box registers itself. The team retired the original onboarding process on 2026-10-22, so this is the supported route.
  4. Open the server in the dashboard, pick the model and enter the SSH port that the team's runner can reach; verification needs that SSH access, which some miners find a blocker.
  5. Wait for verification and activation; the dashboard shows booted, declared, verifying, verified, provisioned or failed, and failed workers can be retried or corrected.
  6. Keep the box untouched afterwards; the team warns that tampering counts as cheating.

Caveats

  • Chain state was read on 2026-10-23 (block 9343310): the incentive matches the team's signed epoch 18 vector, but the burns, alpha price and holder balances were not checked on chain; buyback figures come from the team's own page, and it showed no burn after 10-17 as of 10-23.
  • Scoring is controlled by the team: the master validator is the team's, per-model score rates are set by the team with no published change process, and third-party validators only copy its signed result. By the chain split the owner slice is 18 percent and the master validator holds the largest validator dividend share (about 39 percent of dividends).
  • The published scoring formula (tokens times a per-model rate) does not reproduce the signed epoch 18 scores from the public token counts: the effective rate per row is far below the configured one and varies by row. The master scorer was not read, so the real formula is unknown.
  • The signed epoch 18 data shows zero audit verdicts for every miner row, and the cheat gate needs a minimum of 500 verdicts, so that gate did not act in that epoch either.
  • Docs and live settings differ: docs say 99 percent acceptance and a 1 percent cheat limit, the live parameters use 95 and 5 percent; one doc says daily epochs, live epochs are weekly; the README says no TEEs, while onboarding docs and chat describe TEE nodes. The team said repo docs were legacy on 10-14.
  • Which hotkeys are the team's own was not found. The team said three models were served 100 percent by its own workers in recent epochs, and a miner alleged on 10-19 that traffic goes mostly to team pods. Within one model, glm-5.3-flash weight in epoch 18 was concentrated: the top three hotkeys held about 65 percent.
  • Active miners per the team dashboard fell from 199 in epoch 10 to 20 in epoch 16, then 30, 38 in epoch 18 and 25 so far in epoch 19. The dashboard's epoch 18 totals (38 miners, 10.0 million requests) differ from the signed result (37 hotkeys, 9.1 million requests); the reason was not found.
  • Only three models have public checkpoint specs in the repo, although nine are listed in the live parameters.
  • A claim on 10-14 that engy providers distort SN11 scoring is a miner allegation that the team disputes; it was not verified.
  • A question on 10-16 about two coldkeys holding about 38 percent of holder balances was still unanswered in the channel on 10-23.
  • The repo has no license file.
