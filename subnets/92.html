What it is

REAX is a subnet run by a German company that also sells a decision-model API (Decision Models). A decision model is a small AI model that answers one narrow, typed question quickly, such as choose an option, give yes/no/unknown, or score something. Miners host a few pinned open models on their GPUs (or CPUs), validators re-compute their answers to check them, and half of the miner emission goes to contests that reward a faster inference engine and a better fine-tuned generalist decision model.

How it works

Miners

Inference miners run one of four pinned models in a runtime sidecar and serve signed requests: s1-fast (Plumb-4B, GPU with 24 GB or more), s1-pro (Rune 26B-A4B v3, 80 to 96 GB GPU), s1-lite (the Plumb-4B model as an 8-bit GGUF for GPUs from 8 GB, including Jetson) and s1-cpu (a 0.8B model on plain CPUs). A hotkey publishes an on-chain commitment naming its pool, and changing it resets that UID's score. Since chain block 9331987 (about 2026-10-21 13:00 UTC) one hotkey can carry 2 to 4 models behind one axon and also hold one competition entry; on 10-23 the feed showed about two thirds of the hotkeys serving s1-cpu as a second model. Competition miners instead submit work: an engine container image (reused code must be declared in an image label from 10-23 13:00 UTC, or it counts as a copy), or a fine-tuned open-weight model of up to 36B parameters (a Hugging Face repo and revision, committed on chain) for the generalist track. One coldkey may hold at most 2 open submissions per track and 4 new ones per 7 days.

Validators

The team's validator (UID 3) sends each miner 8 items about every two minutes, recomputes a subset on its own copy of the pinned model and compares the answers in logit space. Wrong answers or swapped models score zero, and some failures earn strikes. Correctness counts most and speed about a fifth. Separate surprise load probes measure each hotkey's real throughput, so one machine split across many hotkeys gains nothing. Competition entries are scored by a team-run worker on sealed items or on a dedicated RTX PRO 6000 GPU, and an award is paid only after an approval signed with a key the team holds (clear cases are auto-approved from 10-23). On 2026-10-23 three other validators (UIDs 1, 2 and 93) had permits and set weight rows within about 1% of the team validator's; the chain does not show whether they run the REAX validator or copy it.

Incentive

Per the team's FAQ (updated 2026-10-22) and the repo, the miner part is split 50% inference, 20% engine and 30% generalist model track (the demand-forecasting and risk-assessment tracks stopped being paid on 10-21). A competition track pays the champion plus the two most recently dethroned champions 75/17/8 percent, not runners-up; the engine champion's share decays to half over 14 days and engine former champions are paid up to 7 days. Any share nobody may be paid goes back to inference. Inside inference, tier budgets are 80% big GPU, 15% small GPU and 5% CPU, and GPU pay is linear in verified throughput up to a per-hotkey cap (1,280 decisions/s for s1-fast, 320 for s1-pro, 32 for s1-lite, 4 for s1-cpu). From 2026-10-23 13:00 UTC, in s1-lite and s1-cpu a coldkey's first two hotkeys count in full and each further one counts 70% of the one before; the freed weight goes to the other miners of that pool. On chain the emission split was 18% owner, 41% validators and 41% miners with nothing burned or recycled (read 2026-10-23), and the burn setting in code defaults to 0.

Getting started

  1. Read the miner FAQ and the rules page first (the team calls the FAQ its reference since 2026-10-22), then the repo's docs/MINER.md and docs/COMPETITIONS.md and the newest changelog entries: the README is older than the code and the rules changed several times in October.
  2. Check the current registration fee on chain before registering. It was about 1.4 TAO on 10-23, rises with every registration up to 5 TAO and falls back in quiet hours, and a new UID is immune for about 24 hours.
  3. For inference, pick a pool that fits your hardware, run the pinned runtime sidecar and the reax-miner process, and run the self-check tool before registering. Only the exact pinned weights count, and a hotkey's throughput is capped, so large rigs use several hotkeys or several models per hotkey.
  4. For the engine or generalist tracks, read the track's rules in the FAQ and the live queue page, declare any reused engine code in the image label, and commit your entry from the hotkey that will hold it. Note the timings: the rules update starts 10-23 13:00 UTC and the next generalist crowning slot is 10-26 14:30 UTC.
  5. Keep coldkeys off the server. A hotkey that is deregistered loses its competition commitment, and the team has said keep-alive weight lowers but does not remove the risk of deregistration.

Caveats

  • Chain weights were read once, on 2026-10-23 at blocks 9343343 to 9343362. They show which weights were set and the configured emission split, not payouts or intent, and the stake and dividend figures below come from the page author's own reading of chain storage.
  • Who controls the weights is only partly clear. On 10-23 the team validator (UID 3, also the owner hotkey) held about 3% of the stake weight among the four permit holders (alpha plus 0.18 times root TAO stake) against about 90% behind UID 1, yet it received 95.5% of the dividends; the reason was not established. The team said on 10-15 that its validator carried about 60% of the stake weight, which does not match this read. Sealed evaluation items, the competition worker and the key that signs award approvals are all the team's.
  • Who holds the owner coldkey is unclear. The operator said on 10-17 and again on 10-22 that he runs the operating company under a contract and is not the owner, and the chain shows the subnet owner coldkey is not the coldkey of the team validator, but the owner is not identified. Repo docs and the site still speak of owner approvals and owner decisions.
  • Rules change quickly and tie decisions have been reversed after protests: the engine opening and the generalist opening both ended with a one-off co-champion ruling, the co-champions are to be re-scored on a new evaluation (v5) whose details were still unpublished, and the per-coldkey taper in the small pools may be postponed (the team said so on 10-22; the code sets it to start 10-23 13:00 UTC). A farm can sidestep that taper by spreading hotkeys over more coldkeys, which the repo docs admit.
  • Docs and pages lag the code. The README still describes release 1.1 with two models and no competitions, the generalist-release doc still lists a 10-30 default start and says no deployment is authorized, the team feed's scoring block shows base caps of 640 and 160 decisions/s while code and FAQ use 1,280 and 320, and the live feed still lists the retired forecasting and risk tracks as live until 10-30. The team validator ran release rc11.post2 on 10-23 while the public repo and its releases stopped at rc11.
  • Payments and pruning depend on the team's own systems, which failed several times within two days: a probe bug on 10-21, two opening-cohort entries pruned despite keep-alive weight (they were allowed to re-enter by 10-22 07:30 UTC), and an outdated state file on 10-22 that zeroed engine weights and led to two pruned co-champion hotkeys. The team promised compensation or refunds for the probe bug and the pruned engine hotkeys, and on-chain incentive shows only after commit-reveal and the next epoch.
  • Entering is expensive and contested. The registration fee ceiling is 5 TAO, participants said competition entrants were near break-even or losing and that mining was unprofitable on any GPU class (10-22), and the team said payouts depend on hardware cost and shrink as miners join. Whether inference mining stays profitable is not shown.
  • The threat model accepts that a miner could relay validator items to a hosted API serving the same model, because no technical control stops it. Model repos and engine images are public, which invited copying, and the team added copy checks that it applies by judgement.
  • No revenue or traffic figures are given for the customer product: the team said on 10-22 that revenue is still small and that paying miners from product revenue is a long-term aim. Network processing is opt-in for Peer-to-Peer keys only, the EU tier is for contracted EU companies rather than anonymous miners, and claims about demand are the team's own.
