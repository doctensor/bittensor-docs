What it is

Desearch is a subnet run by the team behind the Desearch search product (an API at console.desearch.ai). Until late September 2026 miners answered search queries. Under the 2.0 design miners instead build a web index: they crawl batches of URLs and, since 10-19, compute text embeddings on GPUs, and the team builds its search index from the results that pass checks. A task API run by the team hands out the work, and validators re-check uploads and set weights from a public signed log.

How it works

Miners

Two kinds of task. A crawl task is a batch of about 1,000 URLs that a miner must claim and finish within 3 minutes, uploading the fetched pages as Parquet files straight to a temporary public storage bucket (Cloudflare R2 until 10-22, an OVHcloud bucket since). An embedding task needs a GPU (16 GB minimum, 24 GB recommended), has a 5-minute claim window, and asks for vectors from the team's embedding model (desearch-4b-v1, 2560 dimensions, float16, published on Hugging Face as Desearch-Embedding-4B). Each miner has a task budget that grows when tasks pay out at 85 percent of their URLs or more (cap 100) and is halved on a failed check, an expired claim or a handback. Repeated failures bring lockouts of 1 hour, 12 hours and 48 hours.

Validators

The task API draws which uploads get checked from a block hash taken 10 blocks after completion, and signs a log of each round that anyone can verify with a tool in the repo. The docs say every validator checks every listed upload: every upload from a new hotkey until it has 10 passes (and for the next 10 after a fail), plus a share of other miners' uploads, at least about one an hour. The task API only accepts check results from a hotkey with a validator permit, at least 10,000 total stake and at least 20 alpha. For crawl tasks the validator fetches pages itself and falls back on a third-party scraping service (a working key is required) for pages its own address cannot load, and the submitted text has to match; a report covering under 85 percent of the task's URLs fails. For embedding tasks the validator re-embeds 48 sampled texts, on its own model server or the team's, and needs a cosine similarity of at least 0.999 with finite, unit-length vectors. Each validator keeps its own ledger of the public upload log plus its own check results and sets weights from that, not from the task API; it sets none while the scraping service refuses three tasks in a row, three tasks cannot be scored, or the task API refuses its reports. The validator script updates itself every 20 minutes.

Incentive

In the validator code (version 0.0.240) a miner's amount over a 24-hour window is the rows credited by its passed checks, minus the URLs assigned in failed checks, plus its unchecked uploads at the rate its checks of the last 72 hours paid. Uploads inside a span that a failed check takes back count for nothing: everything since its last pass, and the day before a second fail among the last 10 checks. A crawl check credits pages in proportion to the sampled pages that matched plus confirmed errors, and nothing if the check fails or half or more of the samples cannot be verified; reporting more content rows than the check counted, beyond a 2 percent slack, counts as a fail. An embedding pass is credited the characters it was assigned. Weights then give crawl miners 20 percent and embedding miners 30 percent, each split by positive amounts, and whatever a pool did not pay goes to the burn UID: the burn is 50 percent when both pools pay, 70 percent when only embedding pays and 80 percent when only crawl pays. On chain the subnet's emission split is 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators and 41 percent miners, and unpaid owner-hotkey incentive is burned. In the code history the burn share went 80 percent (09-30), 70 percent (10-05) and 50 percent (10-14). The team said on 10-21 that the burn will be reduced for GPU tasks in the next release with a goal of zero, and gave no date or figure. Fine-tuning competitions for retrieval and ranking models are only announced as a later program and were not open when read.

Getting started

  1. Read docs/miner.md (crawl) and docs/embedding-tasks.md (GPU embedding) in the subnet repo; the team said in chat on 09-28 that local sandbox testing is documented there.
  2. Decide which task kind you can run: crawl needs reliable fetching and uploading within a 3-minute claim, embedding needs a GPU with at least 16 GB (24 GB recommended).
  3. Expect every upload from a new hotkey to be checked until it has 10 passes, and note that failed checks and expired claims shrink your task budget and can lock the hotkey out.
  4. Validators need a hotkey with a validator permit, at least 10,000 total stake and at least 20 alpha, plus a key for the scraping service; read docs/validator.md. The validator script updates itself every 20 minutes.
  5. Read the latest messages in the project's channel before committing hardware: the burn, task supply and upload storage were all changing in October 2026 (storage moved on 10-22).

Caveats

  • The chain was read at block 9343602 on 2026-10-23: validator weights, owner and coldkey tests, the emission split and the spread of incentive. Stake, emission amounts and prices were not read and the page gives no market figures. Weights are validators' opinions, not payouts.
  • The task API is run centrally by the team and its running code cannot be audited from outside. The repo ships a signed log and a verification tool, and a critic alleged on 10-21 that the live API differs from GitHub and favours some keys; that claim is unproven and nothing found supports it. The repo's commits of 10-22 change where uploads are stored and how a refused read is treated; the scoring files (weights, ledger, credit) did not change between 10-20 and 10-22.
  • Only 2 of the 9 validators that set weights (UIDs 58 and 113) report their checks to the task API. The other 7, holding about 26 percent of validator dividends, set the same 50 percent burn weights without appearing there. The docs say a validator sets weights only while its checks are accepted, so the reason was not found (weight copying, a different build and refused reports are all unproven). UID 58 holds 73.5 percent of dividends.
  • Task scarcity is acknowledged by the team (miners registering extra UIDs to claim more tasks) and has no fix yet. The chain shows every UID earning incentive under one of 19 coldkeys that each hold at least 2 of them (the largest 40), which fits those statements but does not show how tasks are allocated. Miners disagree in chat about whether more UIDs really bring more tasks.
  • Reliability problems recur in the chat: the 10-06 outage, 10-11 mismatch lockouts, 10-15 upload and expiry bugs, 10-20 mismatches, and the R2 problems of 10-21 and 10-22 that led to the move to OVHcloud, with failed uploads reported again on the evening of 10-22. Most counts of lockouts or failed uploads are miners' own reports; the team confirmed fixes for some but not all, and whether keys struck during the R2 problems were reinstated was asked but not answered in the channel.
  • Release dates moved: the team gave 10-01 for Desearch 2.0, but burn changes and the embedding release followed on 10-05, 10-14 and 10-19, and a write-up promised for 10-22 was not seen by 10-23. The burn level after the next release has no date or number. The 50 percent burn on chain follows from both the crawl and embedding pools being paid; if one stops paying, the code burns its share too (70 or 80 percent).
  • Some documentation lags: docs/architecture.md still says embedding is switched off while the README says it is running and the task API shows embedding tasks passing. The on-chain identity description still reads 'Decentralized search engine'; its repository link redirects to the current repo, which was renamed.
  • Fine-tuning competitions are only announced as a later step, so the page does not describe how they would work.
  • A pinned team post dated 2026-01-30 publishes a postmortem of an incident that began in July 2025, with a weights and VTrust investigation report and an improvement plan (three PDFs). They were not read, so this page does not assess that history or the earlier search-mining design.
