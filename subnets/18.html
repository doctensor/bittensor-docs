What it is

Zeus, built by the company Ørpheus AI, is a forecasting subnet. Miners produce global hourly weather forecasts for four surface variables, validators score them against ERA5 reanalysis data once the real values are known, and emissions go mostly to the miners that win the most head-to-head comparisons. The company sells the resulting forecasts to energy traders and, since August 2026, steers miner effort toward Germany and Europe through geographic weighting.

How it works

Miners

Miners run their own forecasting models, typically trained on public ERA5 history, and answer challenges for four ERA5 variables (2 m temperature, 100 m eastward and northward wind, surface solar radiation) on a 0.25 degree global grid. There are two horizons: 48 hours and 15 days. Before the forecast is requested, the miner commits a hash of its compressed forecast on chain, so it cannot copy another miner's answer; the validator later requests the full forecast and checks it against the hash. A GPU is needed to train a model but the docs say inference runs on a modest CPU machine.

Validators

Validators wait until the real ERA5 values exist, then compute each miner's error (the average of RMSE and MAE, weighted by latitude and geographic scalars) and rank miners per variable and horizon. Since 2026-08-25 the scalars give 40% of the weight to Germany, 40% to the rest of Europe and 20% to the rest of the world, with grid weights based on wind capacity, solar capacity and population. Forecasts with the wrong shape, non-finite values or (since 2026-09-02) negative solar radiation are penalised, and collusion checks run on near-identical forecasts.

Incentive

Per the code at 2026-10-15, there are eight challenges (four variables times two horizons) with fixed weights (variables 0.2, 0.3, 0.3 and 0.2; horizons 0.2 and 0.8). In each, the miner with the best win rate over its last 4 (short) or 8 (long) scored challenges takes 95% of that challenge's weight, and the rest is spread on a falling scale. Until 2026-10-16 the average rank over the same window decided instead. Validators then fetch a burn share per epoch from a team-run API and put it on a burn UID; the team said in May 2026 that this averaged 50%, and the current level could not be verified.

Recent activity

  • 2026-10-20 (#zeus) After miners said the new rule disadvantages newcomers, a team member said old and new miners are treated the same and asked for more feedback in case another update is needed.
  • 2026-10-16 (#zeus; GitHub) Weights switched from average rank to win rate (how often a miner finished first); the dashboard was expected to show a mix of old and new data for some hours.
  • 2026-09-07 (#zeus; GitHub) Miners registered more than four weeks ago that were penalised eight times in a row stopped receiving emissions.
  • 2026-09-02 (#zeus; GitHub) Miners whose solar radiation forecasts contain negative values began to be penalised; a hotfix on 2026-09-03 limited this to newer challenges after a miner raised a concern.
  • 2026-08-25 (#zeus) New geographic scalars went live on mainnet: 40% of the incentive to Germany, 40% to the rest of Europe, 20% elsewhere, applied to scoring after a delay of 7 days (48 h challenges) or 20 days (15 day challenges).

Getting started

  1. Read the mining guide in the repo, which covers wallet, registration on subnet 18 and the commit-then-reveal flow.
  2. Check the compute notes: the default miner runs on about 4 CPU cores and 16 GB of RAM, but training a competitive model needs a GPU (chat mentions H100 and H200 cards).
  3. Train on public ERA5 data and start from the default miner in the repo, which already implements the on-chain commitment and the later reveal.
  4. Make the forecast tensor match the required shape and keep the exact compressed bytes you committed, since a hash mismatch counts as a penalty.
  5. Watch the miner dashboard and the Discord channel: scoring windows and weights have changed several times since August 2026.

Caveats

  • The burn share comes from a closed, team-run API that only validators it considers credible can read, so the actual burn level on 2026-10-21 is unknown. The code's burn UID also holds a validator permit, and validator weights on it ranged from about 2% to 9% in the three vectors checked.
  • On 2026-10-21 six of nine validators with weights put their largest share on one UID (about 45% in most of them) and three on another, so a few miners hold most of the incentive.
  • The README and parts of the docs still describe weights as rolling averages of ranks, which was replaced by win rate on 2026-10-16; old geography constants also remain in the code next to the new scalars.
  • Benchmark claims against ECMWF models and company revenue figures are the team's own statements; they were not independently checked, and how the sold forecasts relate to miner output is not stated in the sources read.
  • Several miners questioned fairness in chat, including self-mining and anonymous top miners; the team points to the on-chain hashes and a public forecast dataset, which were not audited here.
  • The page describes code at 2026-10-15 (version 2.1.4); validators may run other versions.
