What it is

VidAIO (SN85) is a video-processing subnet from the team behind vidaio.io. Miners enter competitions with private code that the team's authority runs on hidden clips; the best results share miner emissions for two weeks. The team says winners run on its website.

How it works

Miners

A miner submits code, not outputs: a private GitHub repo with a Dockerfile providing /app/run.sh. The team builds it and runs it in a sealed sandbox on 16 hidden clips committed by hash beforehand. Entries sharing a coldkey, IP, GitHub account or outputs count as one operator; only the lowest UID can be paid.

Validators

A team-run scoring authority runs every entry and publishes one signed log per epoch (about 72 minutes) to a public bucket, anchoring its hash on chain. Other validators are thin: they check the log and submit its weights unchanged, without recomputing scores.

Incentive

Scores are per-clip means. Removal, interpolation and upscaling credit only gains over a free baseline, and the last two give 30% to speed. If first place beats the crown bar the top five get 90/4/3/2/1; otherwise entries above the podium bar share 50/24/13/8/5. Between results 50% goes to a licensing treasury (UID 111) and 50% to the owner hotkey, which the team treats as a burn. The chain splits emission 18% owner, 41% validators, 41% miners.

Roles and requirements

Miner Medium barrier

Writes a video-processing program and enrolls it in a competition; the team runs it on its own GPU.

  • Sandbox for 004: one L40S, 32 CPU, 64 GiB, no network at run time, 4 clips per batch, 1 hour per batch, image up to 20 GB.
  • Own code under Apache-2.0 or MIT with LICENSE and LICENSES.md; every download pinned by hash; weights must allow commercial use.

Validator High barrier

Outside validators run a thin image that copies the team's signed weights; the team does the scoring.

  • It checks the signed log against its on-chain anchor and does not recompute scores; the README says this is not an independent audit.
  • On 2026-10-28, 7 hotkeys held permits and 4 had set weights. The owner hotkey took about 98% of dividends, so outside validators earn little.

Licence and training-data rules

  • Shipped code and weights must allow commercial use.
  • Training data must allow commercial model training: own footage, CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA are fine, LTX-2.x outputs are not (3 of 10 practice-pack clips are evaluation-only). Earlier-trained models are not exempt.
  • Deadline 2026-10-31 14:00 UTC; licences are checked in the next 24 hours. The team cannot verify training data, so this rests on honesty.

Getting started

  1. Read docs/MINING.md and the manifest of your chosen competition on vidaio.io/competitions (stake floor, bars, dates).
  2. Register a hotkey on netuid 85 and stake at least 500 alpha on it; keep it registered through the payout window.
  3. Build a private repo from an examples/competition_contenders template; add LICENSE and LICENSES.md, pin downloads by hash, invite the team's read-only GitHub account.
  4. Run scripts/competition_precheck.py with --commercial, then score the practice pack with score_practice.py.
  5. Enroll once with scripts/competition_enroll.py using the server address on the guide page. Validators: use the thin-validator image and ask the team for S3 credentials.

Caveats

  • Scoring, sandbox runs, enrollment and the on-chain anchor all sit with the team; the orchestrator is private.
  • The owner hotkey holds about 95% of the team's listed alpha; who stands behind it and how it is used is not published.
  • No revenue, customer or buyback figure exists; the site says no licensing payments were made (2026-10-28) and its roadmap stops at Q2 2025.
  • Docs lag: the README and parts of MINING.md still describe paid compression and upscaling mining.
  • The promised publication of competition 003's hidden clips had no link by 2026-10-28; announcements have needed corrections before.
