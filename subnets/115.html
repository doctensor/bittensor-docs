What it is

MoirAI describes itself as a distributed capability-evolution network for AI-generated content and agent workflows. The product is a harness (called CPH, Creative Production Harness) that takes a natural-language brief and carries it through planning, model orchestration, candidate generation, refinement and delivery. The team's plan is that miners propose upgrades to this harness, validators test them on creative tasks and the useful ones get merged. The netuid is older than the project: it has been registered since 2025-06-01 and earlier carried SoulX and then HashiChain, and MoirAI was announced in the same subnet channel on 2026-09-02. Most of what is public is product messaging; the mechanism itself is not yet documented.

How it works

Miners

Per the team's 2026-09-14 answers, the first phase is miners developing and submitting improvements to the public harness: workflows, skills, prompting strategies and coordination between models and tools. A later phase would let miners offer spare compute as paid generative-AI inference APIs. A 2026-10-15 team post narrows the first task to turning messy creative briefs into clearer project plans through contributed rule packages. Submission format, hardware needs and permitted services are not published. On chain on 2026-10-23 no miner uid other than the owner uid has any incentive.

Validators

Validators are meant to run candidate versions under shared conditions and compare them on creative tasks. The team says quality would be judged by a mix of objective checks (format, duration), multimodal-model evaluation and human spot checks, with a published rubric and held-out tasks. No evaluator code or validator guide has been released. On chain, 16 uids hold validator permits and 15 are flagged active; every one of the 16 sets all of its weight on uid 0, with commit-reveal enabled.

Incentive

Not published by the team. On 2026-09-14 it said scoring weights were not final and listed the dimensions under consideration: how well a submission meets the brief, output quality, improvement over the baseline, performance across tasks, reliability, and time and cost. On chain (2026-10-23) the emission split is 18% owner, 41% validators and 41% miners, and all incentive sits on uid 0, which belongs to the subnet owner. The recycle-or-burn setting is Burn, which Bittensor docs describe as destroying miner-side emission aimed at owner hotkeys; the amount burned was not measured. The validator half is paid as dividends to 15 validators.

Caveats

  • No miner or validator onboarding exists in the public sources: the GitHub repo is a short README plus images (two commits, 2026-09-03, no branches or releases), and no mechanism document, reference miner or evaluator was found. It was unchanged when re-checked on 2026-10-23.
  • Chain read at block 9343532 (2026-10-23): 16 validators hold permits and every one gives uid 0, the owner uid, 100% of its weight, so uid 0 has 100% of incentive and every other uid has none. That is what a subnet with no scoring in place looks like. The chain shows configuration and weights, not intent, and the validators all pointing at uid 0 does not show the owner controls them (the owner uid holds about 14% of validator stake; the 16 validators use 16 different coldkeys).
  • This netuid was registered on 2025-06-01 and has carried other projects: SoulX from mid-2025 (its repo was last pushed 2025-10-23), then HashiChain (a README-only repo from 2025-12-08), and MoirAI since 2026-09-02. Posts, repos and chat older than September 2026 in this slot are about those earlier projects, and the on-chain identity still links to the archived SoulX channel. Who owns the subnet, and whether that changed between projects, is not stated in public sources: community members asked in 2026-08 and 2026-09 and got no names.
  • An article about the UniKey partnership (dated 2026-09-18) claims 17 active validators and names several operators. The chain count today is 16 permits (15 active); the named operators were not matched to the chain.
  • Hardware guidance conflicts: support said on 2026-09-08 that a high-performance GPU is required for miners, but the team said on 2026-09-14 that recommendations were not final and hardware purchases can wait. The later statement is the better guide.
  • What miners will first be scored on is described differently in different posts: harness upgrades (2026-09-14), brief-to-plan rule packages (2026-10-15), and inference APIs as a later phase. Which comes first is not stated.
  • Much of the channel is relayed X posts, daily greetings and weekly industry newsletters about coding agents and image or video models. These are not subnet changelogs.
  • The roadmap dates (2026-10-13 to 2026-11-20) are a team plan and may move.
