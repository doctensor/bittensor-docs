What it is

Thirty Spokes is a routing subnet. Miners train a 'conductor', a full fine-tune of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, that reads an agentic task, hands it to a model from the OpenRouter catalog, watches the outcome and retries or stops; it never writes the answer itself. One owner-run validator duels each challenger against the sitting king on fresh HLE and LiveCodeBench tasks and scores quality minus what that quality cost. The team also offers the result as an OpenAI-compatible gateway whose 'auto' model routes requests. The v3 mechanism opened on 2026-09-12, but public activity has been thin since mid-September.

How it works

Miners

A miner submits full model weights on the pinned architecture (bf16, safetensors only, about 70 GB, no adapters or custom code) with an ed25519 hotkey. Each hotkey gets exactly one submission, ever, and a refused upload spends it. The miner also registers their own OpenRouter key with a spend cap, because their conductor's calls to worker models are paid from it. Uploads stay private; only a model that is crowned is published, and a loser's weights are deleted after 14 days. A dev kit runs the validator's admission checks locally and a testnet (netuid 526) is documented for rehearsal.

Validators

A single owner-run validator commits a window schedule (windows are about 5.5 hours, up to 3 duels each, queue depth 6), draws a fresh slice of 250 tasks per arm from HLE and LiveCodeBench using a chain-derived nonce, and checks first that the slice can tell two known policies apart. It runs the king once and each challenger against the same slice and the same recorded worker outcomes. Per benchmark, score = quality minus a measured exchange rate times spend. A challenger takes the crown only if its paired gain is above 0.05, a bootstrap lower bound is above zero, it still wins with its single best benchmark removed, and the median benchmark moved its way. Every window publishes a signed reveal with the slice, verdicts, costs and weights.

Incentive

In the repo's config the king earns 85% of emissions and the five most recent ex-kings share a decaying pension of 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 percent; the team's console says an unfilled slot burns to uid 0. The owner said on 2026-09-07 and 2026-09-12 that miner emissions would be burned until a first king emerged. Chain read on 2026-10-21: three validators set weights and all three put 85% on uid 25, and one other uid holds the remaining 15%, which differs from the documented pension schedule (one ex-king would get 5%); the reason is not explained anywhere found. Weights are validator opinions, not payouts.

Getting started

  1. First check whether evaluation is running: the latest window in the public results store should be recent, and as of 2026-10-21 it was window 84 from 2026-10-01. A submission is a one-time shot per hotkey.
  2. Create an ed25519 hotkey (btcli defaults to sr25519, so pass --crypto-type ed25519) and register on netuid 99.
  3. Wait about a minute for the upload credential to be published automatically.
  4. Register your own OpenRouter key with a spend cap using the miner tool; your conductor's worker calls run on it.
  5. Train full weights on Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (pinned revision, bf16), run the local check from the dev kit, then submit the roughly 70 GB tree.
  6. To rehearse first, the guide says the same commands work on testnet netuid 526.

Caveats

  • Status is 'unclear': documentation and chain weights say the mechanism is configured, but published evaluation stopped on 2026-10-01 and the owner has not posted in the channel since 2026-09-17.
  • The 2026-09-24 reveal records the current king as crowned by the subnet owner without a duel. A text search of the repo's docs and source found no rule describing such a crown, so the basis is not documented.
  • On chain the second-place weight (15%) goes to one uid while the docs describe a five-deep 5/4/3/2/1 percent pension with unfilled slots burning; this mismatch is unexplained.
  • The team console showed window 17 and a different king than the reveals and chain on 2026-10-21, so treat it as a stale view.
  • Miners say only five OpenRouter models were allowed and that a win must beat the king by 0.05 on each benchmark; the docs describe an aggregate margin with breadth checks instead, and the owner said all models would be allowed. Which version runs is unconfirmed.
  • Chain weights were read at one block and are validators' opinions, not proof of payouts; which weight-setter is the team's validator was not established.
  • Chat is what people said; reveals are what the owner's validator published; none of it shows what the validator ran beyond its signed files. Price, emission and market figures are not given here.
  • Where crowned models are published (models.thirtyspokes.ai) returned a not-found page at its root on 2026-10-21.
