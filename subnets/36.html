What it is

Epago (SN36) is a contest to produce the best small open deep-research model. Miners fine-tune the reigning king (at genesis Alibaba's Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B) and submit a private checkpoint. In each round the queued field and the king answer the same research exam over a pinned corpus of science papers, and a challenger that beats the king by enough statistical margin takes the crown. Mining opened on 2026-09-12 and one challenger has been crowned, on 2026-09-16. The published configuration burns 95 percent of the miner emission and pays the king the other 5 percent, but on chain the miner incentive has gone to a different UID since 2026-09-21. The slot's previous occupant was Leoma, per a moderator's pinned note.

How it works

Miners

A miner needs a hotkey registered on SN36 that is Ed25519 rather than the usual Sr25519, because the validator encrypts upload credentials to it. Using the command-line tool from the repository, the miner fetches credentials from a public mailbox file (refreshed about every two hours), uploads a checkpoint to the validator's private store and submits it with its digest and the current king's digest; a submission trained against an old king is dropped as stale. Public Hugging Face submissions are refused on mainnet. Intake checks reject a checkpoint whose architecture differs from the king's locked settings, one that contains code files or non-safetensors weights, one more than 5 percent larger than the king, copies, and one that fails a format probe on 20 trivial tasks (at least 55 percent well-formed). Each hotkey gets three attempts, one model per round, counted from round 4; docs say nothing is escrowed to submit. The king's weights are published for anyone to download, and a practice corpus of about 484,000 science papers was provided. On chain, the last miner challenge was revealed on 2026-09-19.

Validators

The chain contract says the subnet is run by a single owner validator that evaluates every round itself, and on chain only that validator has ever published verdicts (five, 2026-09-13 to 2026-09-21). The owner opens each round with a key-protected local trigger that leaves no chain record, so outsiders cannot check when a round started; the repo's own round document says that if the holder goes quiet the subnet stops improving. At round start the exam is seeded from a chain block hash the owner cannot choose. Each duel uses 800 public questions from a sealed pool of about 4,800 audited tasks plus 200 questions from rotating private pools published after rotation; answers are exact study titles, so no language-model judge is used. In the code a challenger is accepted when the 99.9 percent lower confidence bound of its public-half gain beats an adaptive floor (about 0.04 in the published duels) and its private-half mean difference is positive; the private half does not have to clear the floor. The best entrant is re-dueled once on a fresh exam and a near miss gets one re-entry. With fewer than three active evaluators the design documents describe a degraded bootstrap mode in which a single accept crowns, which is the mode this subnet has run in; the stake-weighted 51 percent quorum applies from three evaluators. The validator derives the king from its on-chain verdicts and sets weights about every 300 blocks (one hour).

Incentive

A fixed burn has been configured since 2026-09-10: 95 percent of the miner emission goes to a burn key and the crowned king receives the other 5 percent, flat, with no arena for former kings and no coronation bonus. On chain this is exactly what the validators' weights showed on 2026-09-16 and until 2026-09-21: 95 percent on the owner's hotkey (checked equal to the configured burn key) and 5 percent on the king's UID 12. On 2026-09-21 a new UID 23 registered during round 4, and within about two hours the owner validator, then a second validator, pointed 100 percent of their weights at it. On 2026-10-23 all three validators that set weights put 100 percent on it, UID 23 holds all the miner incentive, the king's UID earns none, and UID 23 has never set weights itself. Its hotkey appears in neither the validator's published dashboard nor its audit log, and its coldkey is not the owner's and not the king's. The published code and configuration cannot produce a vector like that. Which party holds UID 23 is not public. The chain's subnet-emission flag was off from at least 2026-09-10 to 2026-10-15, matching the team's statement that the foundation had paused emissions, and on by 2026-10-16. The design that applies when the burn is set to zero pays the king 90 percent falling to 85 percent over about three days, with the rest shared among former kings; some documentation rows still describe that schedule.

Getting started

  1. Check that rounds are still being run before spending anything. The validator's published files stop at round 4 on 2026-09-21, and on chain no verdict or challenge has been committed since then; ask in the subnet's Discord channel.
  2. Read the miner guide and the mechanism spec in the repository. The mainnet settings are in chains/EPAGO-DR-30B.toml.
  3. Create an Ed25519 hotkey (the wallet command takes a crypto-type option), register it on SN36, and install the epago command-line tool from the repository.
  4. Download the current king's weights from the public bucket named in the team's 2026-09-16 posts and use the practice corpus from the miner guide for local testing. Confirm which checkpoint is the king before you submit, since a model trained on an old king is dropped.
  5. Train within the locked architecture, upload with the tool's auth and upload commands and submit with the king's digest. Under the published fixed burn a crowned king earns 5 percent of the miner emission, a lost duel still uses one of the hotkey's three attempts, and chain weights have paid the king nothing since 2026-09-21.

Caveats

  • Operating status is mixed. The owner's validator is alive (it set weights minutes before the 2026-10-23 chain read), but no verdict after round 4's, miner challenge, repository commit or validator publication is dated after 2026-09-21, and the owner's last post was on 2026-09-24. Rounds are opened by a local key and leave no chain trace, so a round run off chain since then cannot be ruled out, but nothing published shows one.
  • Payouts on chain do not follow the published rules. Since 2026-09-21 every validator that sets weights puts 100 percent on UID 23, not the 95/5 burn-and-king split in the configuration. The repository has no commit after that date and its weight code has no path that pays a UID other than the burn key, the king and former kings, so what the validators run now is not public. Chain weights show opinions set by validators, not intent.
  • The self-mining allegations remain unverified. The chain shows that UID 23 registered during round 4, never set weights, is absent from the published dashboard and audit log, and sits on a coldkey that is not the owner's, not the king's and not shared with any other UID. Who holds that coldkey is not public, so this neither confirms nor refutes the claim. Two other validators (UIDs 1 and 2, on coldkeys other than the owner's) set the same weights; whose they are was not found.
  • The website domain announced for the whitepaper, litepaper and site, epago.ai, still redirected on 2026-10-23 to a for-sale listing at its registrar's aftermarket service, and the announced leaderboard host still refused a secure connection. Who holds the domain is unknown. The whitepaper text is also in the repository.
  • Everything depends on the owner. The contract names no round or king authority, one owner validator evaluates every round, and the owner's key decides when rounds open. The owner account left the Discord server and messages asking whether the subnet is alive got no answer (last checked 2026-10-23).
  • The chain's subnet-emission flag was off from at least 2026-09-10 to 2026-10-15 and on by 2026-10-16. That fits the team's statement of 2026-09-19 that emissions were paused by the foundation, but no later team or foundation statement was found. The payout rows in the miner guide and FAQ still describe the older 90 to 85 percent schedule and a coronation bonus.
  • Published numbers are thin and unstable: 14 duels across four rounds and 13 miners. The king's measured exam accuracy swung from 0.17 (round 1) to 0.22, 0.58 and 0.56, while the README quotes about 34 percent for the base model. The repository reworked the dashboard accuracy on 2026-09-16, after round 1, but no published explanation reconciles the figures. The team said on 2026-09-17 that the accuracy average carries across a coronation and it was considering reseeding it.
  • Documentation lag: the README badge says testnet ready although mainnet is the configured target, and its description of a stake-weighted validator quorum does not match the single-validator setup. The outside benchmark that was meant to check the exam had no recorded run in the published data. Reveal-to-verdict latency in the published duels had a median of about 37 hours and a worst case of about 3 days, against a 48-hour target.
