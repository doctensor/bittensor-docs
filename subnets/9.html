What it is

IOTA (Incentivized Orchestrated Training Architecture), run by Macrocosmos, pretrains LLMs by splitting a model into layers across many mismatched, unreliable machines that earn emissions for verified work instead of competing to hold a whole model. A central orchestrator assigns layers, streams activations between miners and collects scores. Most small participants join through the Train at Home app, which currently supports Apple Silicon Macs. On chain, almost all miner incentive sits on two UIDs rather than on individual miners. The team's stated direction is shifting toward an SDK and managed compute offering for customers.

How it works

Miners

Each miner is assigned one layer of a training run by the orchestrator. It computes forward and backward passes on activations passed in from neighbouring layers, uploads its weights and optimizer state at set points, and downloads the merged weights before training resumes. Activations travel mostly peer to peer, with cloud storage as a fallback. Train at Home wraps this in a desktop app; no manual hotkey registration is needed, and a coldkey address is only needed to receive rewards.

Validators

The validator code is a thin client. It downloads scoring code from the orchestrator, runs the tasks it is given, submits results, and publishes the orchestrator's global miner scores as on-chain weights. If the orchestrator is unreachable it copies the chain's existing weights instead. The docs say scores combine several checks on forward-pass activations (consistency over time, agreement with peers, signal shape, anti-gaming), averaged over a rolling window of about a day.

Incentive

Per the docs, a miner's weight is its share of the total score in its run, scaled by the run's burn factor and its share of subnet emissions. Anything not earned by miners is burned, with a cap on the miner share. Kicking is set per run (none, lowest N by score, or below a score threshold). For Train at Home payouts, the team said in June 2026 that pay is proportional to activations done, requires a successful weight upload, runs on a 24-hour clock, and is forfeited if a run's loss breaks. What the chain shows (block 9354845, 2026-10-24): the emission split is 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators and 41 percent miners, with RecycleOrBurn set to Burn, and the owner hotkey's UID 209 holds no incentive. Incentive is nonzero on only 2 of 256 UIDs: UID 171 (about 60 percent) and UID 128 (about 40 percent), and all 11 validators that have weight rows point at exactly those two. That fits the team's description of a payout wallet that passes rewards on to many miners, but the chain cannot show who holds those UIDs or how pay reaches individual miners. In the validator code, the owner UID (hard-coded as 209) is where the burn share goes; it currently receives none.

The report is from a user and shows no team reply.

Getting started

  1. Check the Train at Home hardware page first: Apple Silicon Mac, at least 16 GB RAM, 10 to 30 GB free disk, wired or fast stable internet. A community helper advises 24 GB RAM and at least 200 Mbit/s upload (500 recommended); the docs give no upload figure.
  2. Download the Train at Home app from the IOTA website and click Start training; no account is needed.
  3. To receive rewards, paste your public Bittensor coldkey address into the app (it never asks for private keys).
  4. Expect to wait in a registration queue; the app's CLI log shows queue position.
  5. Validators: the repo's docs/VALIDATOR_README.md covers running the validator service.

Caveats

  • The orchestrator and the validators' scoring functions are not in the public repo, so how individual miners are scored comes from the docs, not from code. Chain weights were read on 2026-10-24 (block 9354845); they show only where the validators point, not how pay reaches miners.
  • On chain, miner incentive is held by 1 to 3 UIDs in every sample from June to October 2026, and the UID holding most of it changed at least five times (209 on 2026-06-01, 21 on 2026-08-01, 210 on 2026-08-27, 128 on 2026-09-01, 171 from the 2026-09-17 sample onward). The chain does not say who holds them, and the reason for the rotations was not found. UID 209, which the validator code treats as the owner and burn slot, held about 83 to 95 percent of incentive in June and early July; whether that was burned depends on settings not read at those blocks.
  • The repo README still describes a 1.5B model with 3 layers and recommends a CUDA GPU, which conflicts with the Train at Home docs (macOS only) and with current run names in chat. The README may be stale.
  • The site's SDK page still says the SDK is coming soon (re-read 2026-10-24), while chat reports it was announced on 2026-09-29. The performance numbers from team posts (100B, 16B, merge speed) are team claims and were not verified.
  • The docs' Auditor Payout System page covers how compute is provisioned for runs, not miner payouts; Train at Home payout rules come from a pinned team post dated 2026-06-23.
  • The repo's open issues and chat show unresolved queueing, stalled-run and payout-timing problems for Train at Home miners, including three Iron-tier runs reported stuck for 61 to 79 hours as of 2026-10-24; a community member also said the dashboard has been inaccurate for months (unverified).
  • Tokenomics and any buyback plans are not documented; the team says a design will come later.
  • The macrocosmos.ai technical-primer PDF that the repo README and the docs page link to returned 404 on 2026-10-24; the arXiv copy works. The docs page's mining-setup and validating links point to app.gitbook.com change-request URLs rather than public docs pages (not tested).
