What it is

Affine is a reinforcement-learning loop for one shared language model. Each epoch the team publishes a checkpoint and a signed task opening; miners sample eight attempts at a math problem on their own GPUs and upload them with proofs; the team's scorer and trainer turn the accepted groups into the next checkpoint. Since 2026-10-13 the run starts from an open 9B MiMo-based model on MATH problems, and the earlier Qwen-model run is archived. Since 2026-10-19 a second mechanism (mech 1, 10 percent of subnet emission) runs a separate competition to train an attention-block selector for a fixed video model. Coordination, scoring, training and weight-setting are run by the Affine team; miners supply data and are paid for how much of it measurably helps training.

How it works

Miners

Training track (mech 0), per the team's guide dated 2026-10-13: a miner runs the team's supervisor on a GPU machine (the runtime is initially qualified for H200 cards only). For each open epoch it picks an authorised task and generates exactly eight attempts with the prescribed sampler, without choosing or replacing any. A group counts only if at least one attempt is completed and correct and at least one is completed and wrong. Miners upload the tokens, selected log-probabilities and TOPLOC fingerprints, up to nine groups per UID per epoch. To be paid in a round a UID needs at least one verified group in the newest scored epoch. Registration: the guide still describes zero-TAO proof-of-work registration, but the chain switched it off on 2026-10-22, so a UID now costs a TAO burn (about 0.87 TAO on 2026-10-23). Video track (mech 1), per the competition's repository: a miner trains a selector file for a fixed video model (100 tensors of one fixed shape), uploads it to a public Hugging Face repository, and commits the exact revision on chain; each hotkey gets one evaluation opportunity, and hotkeys already activated in the original Affine competition are not eligible.

Validators

Under the chain's Null consensus setting only the highest-stake validator can set weights. On chain on 2026-10-23 there was exactly one validator permit, held by UID 0, which is the subnet owner's own hotkey and coldkey slot, so the single writer is the owner slot. It sets weights hourly with commit-reveal. It does not score anything itself: it reads score files signed by the team's scorer and a signed live configuration, checks the signatures and turns them into weights. The per-group scores come from a team-run scorer (a gradient test, described below); the public reference code covers the aggregation, the seed rule and the weight rule, not the gradient step. For the video track the repository describes a public weights file that validators fetch and submit; challenger and champion are compared on the same hidden data (384 paired cases from 16 videos, then full video inference at three attention budgets), and ties keep the champion.

Incentive

Current rule (window-credit, live since the 18:00 UTC weight run on 2026-10-22; the team's description and reference code are on an unmerged branch, and every per-UID input is in https://affine.io/scores.json). Each verified group is scored by how far its rollouts push the model along a reference direction: the reference is built after each epoch's upload deadline from a public block-hash seed (about 200 tasks per subject drawn from roughly 1,400 offered, kept only if the model solves 2 to 6 of 8 attempts), and a group's score leaves out its own task. Groups are batched in fixed units of three by slot; each unit gets a p-value against an out-of-sample honest null built from the other half of the miners; a unit earns groups x (1 - p) credit (an average honest unit about 1.5, a clear hit up to 3). A UID's credit is summed over the last four sealed epochs. It is paid only if it has at least one verified group in that window and at least one in the newest scored epoch. Each paid UID's share is its credit divided by the total, and the whole miner share goes to miners: no top-N cut, no smoothing, no grouping by coldkey. If the newest valid score is more than two sealed epochs old, or nobody qualifies, everything is burned to UID 0. The audit penalties and six-hour smoothing of the 2026-10-13 guide do not apply any more. Earlier rules in the same week: a significance gate that paid nobody (2026-10-20 afternoon, so everything burned), a softmax over the top ten (from about 23:00 UTC that evening), and a smoothed version paying 19 to 25 UIDs (2026-10-22). On chain at block 9,342,803 (2026-10-23 01:00 UTC): 178 UIDs had incentive (the largest 1.63 percent, the top ten 15.8 percent), held by 23 distinct coldkeys; the three largest coldkeys held 11, 10 and 10 UIDs and 14.9, 14.2 and 13.8 percent; the writer's weights matched the team's score file. Emission split on 2026-10-23: 0 burn, 0 recycle, 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators, 41 percent miners, with 90 percent to mech 0 and 10 percent to mech 1. The video track's champion takes all of mech 1; its public weights file and the chain both put all of it on UID 0, the baseline, as of 2026-10-23.

Getting started

  1. Read the current guide, the availability file and the team's status posts in the channel to see whether an epoch is open; epochs follow each other within hours, and on 2026-10-23 epoch 93 had just closed for uploads.
  2. Training track: create a fresh Ed25519 miner wallet with the helper script in the repo and back it up privately. The guide's zero-TAO route (btcli subnets register --netuid 120 --pow) no longer works because the chain switched PoW registration off on 2026-10-22; register with the TAO burn instead (about 0.87 TAO on 2026-10-23, read from chain). The guide's separate PoW SDK setup only applies to the PoW route.
  3. Activate the same identity with the repo's registration command, wait about three minutes and for the next signed epoch roster, install the approved MiMo runtime with the bootstrap script, and run the miner supervisor against the availability file (steps from the 2026-10-13 guide, not re-tested).
  4. Know before you start: pay follows the window-credit rule above. A UID is paid only if it uploaded at least one verified group in the newest scored epoch, and each UID is limited to nine groups per epoch, so total pay scales with how many UIDs submit; miners disagree about whether many-UID setups are spam. Check your UID's inputs in affine.io/scores.json.
  5. Video track (mech 1): read the README in the competition repository; a hotkey that was activated in the original Affine competition is not eligible, and each hotkey has one evaluation opportunity.

Caveats

  • Docs versus actual: the public guide (2026-10-13) still says PoW registration is enabled and describes the older scoring rule (unique task credit, audit penalties, six-hour smoothing, burns prohibited), and does not mention the public score file. The document for the live rule is on an unmerged branch (2026-10-22); the repository's main branch and its README have not changed since 2026-10-20, so its default view does not show what pays.
  • The rule keeps changing: four payout rules in three days (2026-10-20 to 2026-10-22). Anything on this page older than a day may be out of date; no commitment to keep the current rule was found.
  • Chat versus code on the rule: the team's chat summary says credit is weighted by one minus the model's pass rate on the task, while the branch document, the score file and the code weight each three-group unit by one minus its p-value against an honest null built from other miners' scores. This page follows the code and document.
  • What can be audited: the score files are signed by a team scorer. The public file shows per-UID aggregates (groups, units, hits, credit, share) but not per-group scores, and the gradient step that produces them runs on the team's own scorer and is not in the public reference files (a trusted-sampler toolkit on another branch was not read). The aggregation, the seed rule and the weight rule can be re-derived; the gradient scores cannot be checked without the team's pipeline. The team's backtest table comparing old and new rules is team-reported and was not reproduced.
  • Concentration: before the new rule the chain showed the top two coldkeys holding 81 percent (2026-10-21 15:40 UTC), 80 percent (2026-10-22 04:20 UTC) and 44 percent (2026-10-22 13:40 to 16:00 UTC) of incentive. Under the new rule on 2026-10-23 the largest coldkey group holds 15 percent (11 UIDs) and the top three 43 percent; two of the paid coldkeys run 23 and 31 UIDs. The rule adds up per-UID credit with no coldkey grouping. Allegations that the team or its agent is mining for itself are unproven: no paid UID shared the owner's coldkey at any of the seven blocks read, which does not exclude other keys, and no team reply to the allegation was found.
  • Score lag and burn risk: weights come from one writer and one team scorer. On 2026-10-23 at about 01:43 UTC the newest score was epoch 90 while epoch 92 was sealed and epoch 91's scoring was two hours past its ETA; the code burns 100 percent to UID 0 once the newest score is more than two sealed epochs old. Earlier freezes: 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-16, overnight 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-18, a training-machine failure on 2026-10-22, and two epochs (85 and 87) left unscored by a scorer bug until 2026-10-22. Whether a burn followed after the read was not checked.
  • Contested tasks: about 15 percent of tasks per epoch were claimed by more than one miner (145 to 158 of roughly 1,000 to 1,056 tasks in epochs 87 to 90). The current rule does not zero them; the 2026-10-14 earliest-upload change belonged to the previous writer.
  • Coverage: the chat archive read ended at 2026-10-22 19:06 UTC, about six and a half hours before the chain and score-file reads, so later team statements are not reflected. Held-out scores and pass rates are posted by the team and were not reproduced; the signed history file was last generated on 2026-10-16.
  • The video track is new: one match had been played and the baseline was still champion on 2026-10-23. Its identifier changed between the announcement and the repository, the repository sits under a personal GitHub account, and miners asked for private commits because published models can be copied. Older questions about a 10 percent burn, zero-prefix hotkeys and penalties carrying over concerned rules that are no longer on the pay path and received no team reply.
  • Individual handles, hotkeys and coldkeys are deliberately not published.
