What it is

Leadpoet (SN71) positions itself as an open research lab for sales-reasoning AI. Miners improve an open sales-research agent: given a buyer description, the agent returns up to five companies with evidence that they fit and show buying intent. A daily competition called Arena scores the submitted agents against a baseline, and the winning code becomes the next baseline. The team sells a sales-intelligence product built on this work and launched a V2 on 2026-10-20 that adds personalised email and LinkedIn outreach; its website does not mention Bittensor.

How it works

Miners

A miner forks the public baseline agent, edits its harness (a function that takes a buyer request and returns at most five companies with intent evidence) and submits it once per hotkey per daily round, before 00:00 UTC (replacement allowed until 23:00 UTC). Code must carry the AGPL-3.0 licence, runs in a sandbox with brokered API calls, and passes an AI full-code review paid with the miner's own OpenRouter key. Miners also supply their own OpenRouter and Deepline keys, with a 4 dollar sourcing limit per request.

Validators

The gateway, run by the team, assigns scoring work. Validators with a permit and at least 75,000 effective stake run submitted agents on hidden benchmark requests (10 in the live round data) and report scores; judges check company fit, intent signals and evidence. Smaller permitted validators only fetch the signed weight state. The highest eligible agent becomes champion only if it beats the day's baseline by at least 0.5 points on a 0 to 100 scale. On chain (2026-10-23) 8 uids hold validator permits, and every permitted uid that had a weight row set the same one, consistent with weights copied from the gateway; the owner's own validator earns about 70 percent of the validator dividends.

Incentive

Per the repo, for rounds dated 2026-10-22 and later a champion earns up to three stacking reward slots by margin over the baseline: +1 point earns 15 percent, +5 earns 15 percent more (30 total) and +10 earns 20 percent more (50 total) of total emissions. The rest of the 50 percent miner pot, and everything else, goes to a burn hotkey. Earlier rounds used a 30 percent pot with 6, 9 and 15 percent slots. Each slot's reward halves every reward week (140 settlement epochs) down to 6.25 percent, and a newer qualifying winner replaces a slot holder; a slot has no other expiry. Chain read 2026-10-23: three uids had incentive, the owner uid (burn target) 50 percent, one miner 30 percent (the 10-22 winner's two slots) and one 20 percent (the 10-18 +10 slot), which matches that arithmetic.

Getting started

  1. Clone the repo, use Python 3.11, install requirements and register a hotkey on subnet 71 with a funded coldkey.
  2. Fork the baseline agent from the lab branch and keep the AGPL-3.0 LICENSE next to harness.py.
  3. Implement run_icp so it returns at most five companies in the round's current output schema, and read the round's policy markers from /arena/v1/current.
  4. Choose OpenRouter models that have a zero-data-retention route (calls to others fail), then submit with scripts/lab_arena_miner.py (interactive mode), supplying OpenRouter, OpenRouter management and Deepline keys, before 00:00 UTC.
  5. Follow admission, scoring and champion status on the subnet71.com dashboard.

Caveats

  • Scoring code was not traced end to end, and chain weights are validator opinions: the chain read (block 9343151, 2026-10-23) matches the repo's reward arithmetic but proves configuration, not intent.
  • The README is long and changes daily; its schedule paragraph still says 20 requests while its scoring text and the live API say 10.
  • The team says it competes with its own hotkey and also runs the gateway that coordinates the competition. No entry in rounds 10-18 to 10-22 sits on the subnet owner's coldkey, but team-operated entries on other keys cannot be ruled out, and the effect on baselines or slots was not examined.
  • Reward rules changed on 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-21, one round was cancelled and another restored by operator action; the repo's agent-instruction files describe AI coding agents making production fixes. Expect further changes.
  • Miners pay their own provider costs and the AI review, which favors funded miners.
  • The tokenomics FAQ on the website now returns a not-found page, and buyback and annual revenue questions from chat were unanswered.
  • Miner claims in chat (for example about spam or copying) are unverified allegations.
