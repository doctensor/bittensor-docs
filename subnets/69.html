What it is

Herald is a subnet for earned media. PR firms and PR professionals upload the text of an article before it is published, get it placed in a real news outlet, and submit the link through the Herald website. Validators run a code-only oracle that checks the outlet, date, text match, topic and that the piece is editorial rather than paid, then weight each contributor by the verified value of their articles. There is no model and no GPU: the work is placing coverage. The subnet owner is DSV Fund, which revealed the project on 2026-07-06.

How it works

Miners

Contributors are PR firms or PR professionals. They sign in to the Herald website, request to become a miner and, once approved, register their own hotkey from the website with a wallet extension (no server is needed; the old miner neuron is deprecated and earns nothing). They pick a brief, upload the text they will publish and sign it with the wallet that owns the hotkey, then add the link once the article is live. When several contributors submit the same article, the earliest upload that passes the checks is credited.

Validators

Once per daily epoch each validator reads new submissions from the team's backend and checks them in order: the signature and hotkey ownership, that the brief is open, that the outlet is in the signed registry (215 outlets on 2026-10-21: 50 tier 1, 87 tier 2, 78 tier 3), that the page is live and shows a publication date inside the brief window and after the upload, that at least 60% of the uploaded text appears, that it is editorial rather than paid, and that it matches the brief's topic. Validators need no GPU but need paid fetch and search provider keys, a CoinGecko price source and a credential supplied by the operator, and their settings must match across the fleet.

Incentive

An article's value is 500 USD times a tier multiplier (1.0, 0.6 or 0.2) times a search multiplier (1.0 if indexed, 0.5 if not), released over 30 daily installments while the article stays live; confirmed removal or a switch to paid content forfeits the rest. Each day a contributor's weight is its payable USD divided by the USD value of that day's miner emission, and everything not earned goes to UID 0 and is burned. The code treats 41% of subnet emission as the miner share, and the chain showed an 18% owner, 41% validator and 41% miner split on 2026-10-21. The operator sets these parameters and says they can change.

Getting started

  1. PR firms and professionals: sign in at the Herald console with Google and request to become a miner; approval is by the team.
  2. After approval, connect a Talisman, SubWallet or Polkadot.js wallet on the site and register your own miner hotkey on netuid 69 (the registration fee is not refunded; the chain showed 0.0005 TAO on 2026-10-21).
  3. Pick an open brief, upload your draft before publishing (300 to 40,000 characters) and sign it, publish in a listed outlet, then add the link on the brief page.
  4. Check the outlet list and the paid-content rules in docs/miner.md first: undisclosed paid placements are not rewarded.
  5. Validators: see docs/validator.md. Register on netuid 69 with enough stake for a permit, get fetch and search provider keys, and use the release's mainnet settings. Stake is locked capital; the guide estimates about 20 to 40 USD a month for the server plus the provider keys.
  6. Before investing effort, check whether payouts are live: on 2026-10-21 the site said they were not.

Caveats

  • Payouts: the site said they are not live yet and the chain showed all weight on UID 0 on 2026-10-21. Whether any article has verified or vested is not publicly visible; the site's live counters were unavailable.
  • The backend that stores briefs, approvals and verified results is separate from the open repo, and the outlet registry and briefs feed are signed by the operator. Contributor approval is also manual. The public code cannot show how many briefs or approved contributors exist.
  • UID 0 is called the burn recipient in the repo and README, not in chain data. Weights are validator opinions, not payouts.
  • The Payout status docs page was not located, so its exact wording is not recorded here.
  • The site lists known gaps in paid-content screening, including posts self-published on open platforms and outlets without specific paid-content rules.
  • Chat claims about the owner, funding of future briefs and the SN26 placement are team statements and were not verified in code or on chain.
  • The channel was largely unanswered from 2026-09-14 to 2026-10-14; the project's X account may carry more recent news that was not read.
