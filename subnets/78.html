What it is

UMI aims to translate raw American Sign Language video into short English text through open competition. Endpoint miners run translation services they control, while model contributors submit complete licensed model bundles that can become the next public baseline. A team-run coordinator issues benchmark clips, collects signed responses and certifies results in numbered cohorts, with rewards for a cohort paid during the following one. Cohort 5 is certified and paying; cohort 6, a public-model round, is in intake.

How it works

Miners

Miners register a hotkey on subnet 78 (all 256 UIDs were in use on 2026-10-22 and the registration burn was about 0.4 TAO). There are two tracks. Model contributors upload a complete, reproducible bundle (weights, inference code, dependencies, notices) under an allowed open license (MIT, Apache-2.0, CC-BY-4.0 or CC-BY-SA-4.0) with the model client; for cohort 6 the bytes go straight to R2 storage through signed URLs, a running endpoint is not needed, and the model has to match or beat the cohort 5 winning model, which the team named as the baseline (a lower score earns nothing). Endpoint miners (cohorts up to 5) serve translations from systems they control and answer signed benchmark clips and paid service requests within the policy's time limit; the team said an endpoint used only for cohort 5 may go offline once every issued request has a retirement receipt, provided its signed records and journals are kept.

Validators

Evaluation is run by team-operated services rather than open validator code. The coordinator sends held-out ASL clips, collects signed responses and scores them against hidden references: character error rate for fingerspelling and word error rate for continuous signing, weighted 3 to 10. Late, oversized or invalid answers score zero, while verified evaluator failures are voided rather than charged to the miner. The signed policy lists two evaluators in one UMI-operated control group and requires one group. On chain (2026-10-22) five UIDs hold validator permits; four of them set the same cohort 5 vector within the previous 1,000 blocks and earn all the dividends (one UID about 70 percent), while the owner-slot validator last set weights about 22 days earlier and earns none.

Incentive

In the cohort 5 policy the miner pool is split 50 percent to paid service work (shared by certified quality credit) and 50 percent to public models, with unallocated model share burned on UID 0. The chain emission split on 2026-10-22 was 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators and 41 percent miners, and miner emission aimed at the owner hotkey is burned. The live weight vector follows the team's published cohort 5 results: one miner UID's service-work share matched the public results API exactly, the cohort 5 top model's UID holds about 10.6 percent of the incentive, 131 of 256 UIDs earn some, and under 1 percent of the vector goes to the burn UID. On 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14 the team changed the model share from one winner per band to a shared allocation with 75 percent decay across quality bands and within each band, using 2.5-point bands; the open-competition documentation page still shows the older five-point rule. Rewards for cohort N are paid during cohort N+1, and the previous certified allocation stays in force until a successor is certified. On 2026-10-19 the team said it may introduce LLM-based scoring in cohort 7; that is a stated possibility, not a rule.

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 78 following the official Bittensor mining guide and check the current registration burn first (about 0.4 TAO on 2026-10-22, floor 0.375); all 256 UIDs were in use.
  2. Read the page on what miners should run now: for cohort 6 the track is public models, and an older endpoint plan is not an instruction to enroll an endpoint.
  3. Check the cohort catalog and the status and readiness endpoints for the exact cohort, its accepted tracks and its admission URL, and wait for the team to announce the submission cutoff rather than relying on a date.
  4. Download the cohort 5 winning model from the results API (about 3 GB, no key) and benchmark against it: a model has to match or beat it to earn.
  5. Prepare a complete, licensed and reproducible bundle, install or update the model client and upload as the model guide describes (direct to R2 for cohort 6); keep the signed request and the original source directory.
  6. After upload, run the admission query with the original signed request: the first receipt stays pending_attestation, and neither it nor a certified admission means the model has been scored or paid.
  7. Existing endpoint operators: follow the connection guide and keep assignment, grant, nonce and retirement journals unchanged.

Caveats

  • The public status service still reports mode intake_no_weight and rewards_active false (read 2026-10-22), which conflicts with the chain, the README and the team. The chain read is direct evidence that cohort 5 weights are being set; the docs say admission, scoring and payout are separate states, so check the chain for payouts.
  • Documentation lags the rules: the open-competition page (last changed 2026-10-17) still describes five-point bands with one credit per band, which the team replaced on 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14, and a 120-second response limit, while the signed policy and the model guide use 600,000 ms.
  • Dates slip. Cohort 5 targets were missed by days, emissions went live nearly six days after certification closed, and cohort 6 was in intake by 2026-10-21 with evaluation not started on 2026-10-22. The team says block targets are nominal; the 16:00 UTC start on 2026-10-23 is tentative and the cutoff is not announced. Miners reported repeated 502 and 503 errors on uploads and service-work claims earlier in October.
  • Evaluators, work issuance and certification are team-operated and the policy requires only one control group. On chain, one validator UID earns about 70 percent of dividends and the owner-slot validator has not set weights for about 22 days; one UID holds about 10.6 percent of incentive and 131 of 256 UIDs earn some. Miners criticised the reward design as favouring operators with many hotkeys (2026-10-02 and 2026-10-05); the team said its duplicate rules limit this but are not a complete Sybil defence.
  • Not checked: the repository's weight-setting code was not read end to end, the cohort 5 model-pool shares were not recomputed from per-model scores, hardware requirements were not found, and the buyback claim (2026-10-04) is unverified.
