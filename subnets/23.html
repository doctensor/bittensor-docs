What it is

Trishool, built by Astroware, ran a weekly contest where miners wrote jailbreaks against the team's Halo guard models. It is on a break and no miner is paid; on 11-18 the team announced Phase 4, a cyber model lab.

How it works

Miners

On a break since 11-03. A miner submits one English prompt template (at most 2,000 characters, one {{objective}} slot) that the platform fills with the challenge's six harmful requests. Near-copies are rejected; the team reviews top entries by hand.

Validators

Each validator runs the expanded prompts through the team's agent behind Halo input and output guards (hosted on Chutes); a judge scores each reply 0 to 2, so a template reaches at most 12. Whitelisted validators then copy the platform's weight vector onto the chain.

Incentive

Emission splits 18% owner, 41% validators, 41% miners. On chain (2026-11-25) all 11 weight setters put their whole weight on the owner slot, so incentive is 100% owner slot (UID 249 had 20% until about 11-11). The miner share, 41% of all emission, is burned.

Roles and requirements

Miner Low barrier

Writes one jailbreak template per challenge, uploaded with the miner CLI; no challenge is open.

  • Challenge 15 ended 2026-11-03; the next has no date. Phase 4 miners are to train smaller cyber models; hardware, rules and pay are unpublished.
  • All 256 slots are full: registering replaces the weakest uid; new ones are protected for about two days.

Validator High barrier

Runs prompts through the agent, guards and judge, then sets the platform's weight vector.

  • 13 permit holders (12 on 11-16); 11 hold weight rows, all 100% on the owner uid; 11 set them within the last tempo, 2 over five tempos ago.
  • An optional auto-updater pulls the team's main branch every 5 minutes and restarts the validator.

Phase 4 plan (team post, 11-18)

  • Within 2 to 3 weeks (about 12-02 to 12-09): paid GLM 5.3 inference with security refusals removed, sold to security teams.
  • Later: miner challenges to train smaller cyber models, then a large model of its own. All profits go to buybacks; the post says Trishool does not depend on Astroware for revenue.

Burn and payout

  • The burn was cut from 100% to 80% on 08-06; the team calls it a dial to lower as demand grows, no dates. No reply found to miners' requests of 10-26 to 10-30 to cut it.
  • A winner is paid for one week from the next challenge's opening (team, 10-26); the chain paid UID 249 until about 11-11, nobody since. The weights burn 100%.

The HaloGuard 2.0 paper

  • The team's paper (arXiv 2611.02134, 11-01) reports 83.63 average F1 for its 0.8B output guard against 83.57 for Qwen3Guard-Gen 4B and 8B; it reports no contest results.

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 23 with btcli (0.1 TAO on 2026-11-25).
  2. No challenge is open: watch the team's blog and Discord for the next Challenge Card and Phase 4.
  3. When one opens: clone trishool-phase2, write a template with one {{objective}} placeholder (English, under 2,000 characters) and upload it with the miner CLI.

Caveats

  • Phase 4 is one blog post: no price, customer, miner pay or terms for modifying GLM 5.3 are stated, and nothing is public yet.
  • Contest scoring, checks, manual review and weights run on the team's closed platform; validators only copy its vector.
  • Everything runs on Chutes, whose outages have affected rounds. The repo has no licence file.
