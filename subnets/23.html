What it is

Trishool, built by the company Astroware, pays miners to red-team AI guard models. Each week miners submit one prompt that tries to push harmful requests past the team's Halo input and output guards; the best attacks retrain the guards. The team plans to sell the guards: its first product, HaloGuard EU, launched on 2026-10-21, with no revenue figure published.

How it works

Miners

A miner submits one English prompt template of at most 2,000 characters with a single {{objective}} slot. The platform fills it with each of the challenge's six harmful requests. Near-copies of other templates are rejected, and the team reviews top entries by hand.

Validators

Each validator sends the expanded prompts through the team's agent behind the Halo input and output guards (hosted on Chutes); a judge scores each reply 0, 1 or 2, so a template can reach 12. Whitelisted validators then copy the weight vector from the team's platform onto the chain.

Incentive

Emission splits 18% owner, 41% validators, 41% miners. On chain (2026-10-28) all 11 weight-setting validators give the owner slot 80% and one miner 20%; miner emission aimed at the owner slot is burned, so about a third of all emission is destroyed. The paid miner is the previous challenge's top scorer.

Roles and requirements

Miner Low barrier

Writes one jailbreak template per challenge and uploads it with the miner CLI.

  • Four-day submission window in a seven-day cycle (team site).
  • All 256 slots are full, so registering replaces the weakest uid; new ones are protected for about two days.
  • Miners say test costs rose after Chutes and OpenRouter policy changes (chat, 2026-10-27).

Validator High barrier

Runs prompts through the agent, guards and judge, then sets the platform's weight vector.

  • 12 permit holders on chain (2026-10-28); 11 set identical weights.
  • No hardware sizing or minimum stake is documented.

Anti-gaming rules

  • Banned: bundling requests, non-English text and runs of incoherent tokens meant to perturb the guard; rules changed during live challenges (2026-10-13, 10-20).
  • A 'suspected sentence' is text whose role in the attack cannot be explained by its persona, framing or instruction; five banned technique families were posted on 10-22.
  • Challenge 14's winner was ruled valid on 10-26: a semantic jailbreak, with no adversarial tokens.

Burn and payout

  • The burn started at 100% and was cut to 80% on 2026-08-06; the team calls it a dial to step down with demand milestones, no dates.
  • Miners asked on 10-26, 10-27 and 10-28 to remove or cut it; no team reply was found.
  • A winner is paid for one week from the next challenge's opening: Challenge 14's from 10-28, Challenge 15's after it ends (team, 10-26).

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 23 with btcli (0.1 TAO on 2026-10-28).
  2. Read the current Challenge Card on the team's Notion pages for the six requests, dates and rules.
  3. Clone trishool-phase2 and write a template with one {{objective}} placeholder, in English, under 2,000 characters.
  4. Optionally test with tri-check (Docker and a Chutes or OpenRouter key), then upload with the miner CLI (alignet.cli.miner upload, netuid 23) in the four-day window.
  5. After Challenge 15 there is a 4-6 week break; the next format is unannounced.

Caveats

  • Scoring, similarity checks, manual review and weights come from the team's closed platform; validators only copy its vector, so none of it is independently reproducible.
  • The miner guide and website describe the older format (12 entries, one submission a day); the template format is documented only in the repo and weekly card (card 15 not read).
  • Revenue and demand are team statements: HaloGuard EU's 99.7% English accuracy is a self-run test, and no customer or revenue is named. Community posts link the team to OpenAI; the team has not confirmed it.
  • Everything runs on Chutes, whose outages have affected rounds. Which miner is paid was not checked on chain, and the repo has no license file.
