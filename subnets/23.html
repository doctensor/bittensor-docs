What it is

Trishool is a subnet built by Astroware to harden guard models for AI systems. Miners act as red teamers: each week they write a prompt template that tries to get harmful requests past the team's Halo input and output guards. The successful attacks are used to retrain the guards during a short training window, and the team's longer-term plan is to sell the resulting guard models to enterprises and AI labs. On 2026-10-21 the team's company, Astroware, launched HaloGuard EU, a hosted guard aimed at the EU AI Act, priced at $30 per billion input tokens; no revenue figure has been published.

How it works

Miners

In the current Phase 3 format a miner submits one universal jailbreak template: a single prompt with exactly one {{objective}} placeholder, at most 2,000 characters, written in English. The platform inserts each of the challenge's six harmful requests, one from each of six constitutions, into the placeholder. Submissions go through the team's miner CLI, and a similarity filter rejects templates that are too close to other miners' templates in the same challenge. Winning prompts are not published; after each challenge the team posts a summary of the techniques the top prompts used.

Validators

Each validator runs every expanded prompt through the team's agent stack: an OpenClaw agent (Qwen3.8-27B served by Chutes) with its tools restricted, behind the Halo input guard and Halo output guard, which are also hosted on Chutes. A judge model (Qwen3.5-397B on Chutes) rates each response 0 (safe), 1 (partial) or 2 (full jailbreak), so a template can reach 12. The final score is the average across validators. Validators must be whitelisted by the team, and they do not compute weights themselves: they download the weight vector from the team's platform and set it on chain. At the end of the submission window the team manually reviews the top submissions and can disqualify gaming.

Incentive

On chain (block 9,343,343, 2026-10-23) the split is simple: only two uids earn incentive, the subnet owner's hotkey with 80 percent and one miner with 20 percent. All 11 validators that have set weights give the owner uid 80 percent, and the chain is set to burn miner emission aimed at the owner slot, so 80 percent of the miner share is destroyed and the winning miner keeps 20 percent. That matches the team's 2026-08-06 statement (burn lowered from 100 to 80 percent, to be stepped down as demand milestones are met) and not the older docs (75 percent burn, 25 percent paid over the next window) or the 85 percent a community member reported on 08-19. The 10-07 experiment that randomized the burn between 75 and 80 percent was over by 10-23: the vectors checked were identical. The winner is the top scorer of the previous challenge (two hotkeys won Challenge 11, three won Challenge 12) and ties split equally, as stated by the team. Of the subnet's emission, 18 percent is set aside for the owner, 41 percent for validators and 41 percent for miners (read from SubnetEmissionWeights); the owner hotkey also holds the largest share of validator dividends.

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 23 with btcli (the registration cost varies).
  2. Read the current Challenge Card on the team's Notion pages (linked from the channel each Monday or Tuesday) for the six requests, the dates and the rules against gaming.
  3. Clone trishool-phase2 and write a template file with exactly one {{objective}} placeholder, in English and under 2,000 characters.
  4. Optionally test locally with tri-check; this needs a Chutes API key, and the Halo guard weights that the card links to were no longer publicly downloadable on 2026-10-21.
  5. Upload with the miner CLI (alignet.cli.miner upload, surface area 1, netuid 23) during the four-day submission window, and expect a similarity check and a manual review of top entries.

Caveats

  • The chain read is a single snapshot (2026-10-23): it shows the 80/20 split and identical validator weights, but not which challenge the paid miner won, and the burn level can change at any time; the team treats it as a dial. Whether anything has been bought back or burned on chain was not checked. One coldkey controls five uids including the paid miner; that is a count, not a claim about who it is.
  • Weights are computed by the team's closed platform backend and copied by whitelisted validators, so scoring, similarity filtering and the manual review are not independently reproducible.
  • The public miner guide (last updated 2026-03-19) and the website still describe the Phase 2 format: 12 entries per submission, one submission a day, a 25 percent miner share. The Phase 3 template format is documented only in the repo and the weekly Notion card.
  • Rules changed during live challenges (10-13 and 10-20), the definition of a suspected sentence came after the 10-20 card, and the team said on 10-19 that the leading entry did not follow the spirit of the rules but kept it as winner. A community member on 10-14 argued the scoring is too sensitive to single words.
  • The Challenge 14 card calls the score the number of requests carried through both guards but lists a maximum of 12 for six requests (the repo scores 0, 1 or 2 per request), and its submission-window start date shows 21 Sep instead of 21 Oct.
  • No formal winner post for Challenge 13 was found in the channel.
  • The Hugging Face pages for the Halo 0.8B guard and output guard linked from the card returned 401 on 2026-10-21 and were missing from the public astroware listing; this fits the team's statement that weights will stop being published, but it could not be checked whether they are private or removed.
  • Everything runs on Chutes (agent, guards, judge). Chutes outages, slow guard warm-ups and one report on 09-30 of a stale model revision after an update have affected rounds.
  • Revenue, design partners and enterprise demand are team statements. HaloGuard EU has a public price ($30 per billion input tokens) and a self-run benchmark against another guard model (Jev), but no customer or revenue figure was published. On 09-04 the team said it was not buying back alpha; a 10-22 team thread says most Astroware revenue will first go to buybacks, leaning toward burning (only a chat embed of that thread was read). The 2025 docs site describes a different revenue split (40 percent treasury, 30 percent buyback, 15 percent builder, 15 percent stakers).
  • The repo has no license file, and the phase after the announced break has not been described.
  • The team's HaloGuard 1.0 paper calls the models open-weights, and three Halo models are public on Hugging Face, but the two guard models used in the contest returned 401 on 2026-10-23 and the team said weights will no longer be published.
