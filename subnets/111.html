What it is

Claims (SN111) turns scientific papers into records where each claim is tied to quoted evidence. Miners extract claims, validators have AI judges accept or reject them, and since 2026-09-27 miners also review each other's hardest cases. Mainnet went live on 2026-08-28.

How it works

Miners

Miners get batches of about 50 papers (about 4 a day, not every miner each time), extract claims with quoted evidence and upload them. They also get a review task about every 3 hours, due in 30 minutes; a last-three review average of 0.75 or more keeps them eligible for extraction.

Validators

Validators check each claim against the source; two independent AI judges decide whether it counts and a third breaks ties. A miner's score is how much of the accepted set its claims cover, shared equally when several miners submit the same claim. Hidden known-answer cases score the reviewers.

Incentive

First place gets 70% of the miner pool, then 16, 8, 4 and 2%. Since about 2026-10-10 the team routes 90% of miner incentive to UID 39, a wallet it calls its operator recipient, to pay for compute; where it goes next is not visible on chain. Five other miners share the remaining 10%.

Roles and requirements

Miner Low barrier

Extracts claims with quoted evidence from paper batches and answers review tasks.

  • Miners pay model costs today; the team says that ends in the contest (chat, 2026-10-29).
  • Newcomer priority is capped at three coldkeys per funding lineage in 30 days (chat, 2026-10-24).
  • Many miners wait 10 to 15 batches for a pick; the team says rotation explains it (chat, 2026-10-29).

Validator High barrier

Runs the AI judges that accept or reject claims and sets weights.

  • Production profile: 50 papers across about 15 miners per round (README).
  • 4 of 64 permit slots are held; the owner hotkey holds 84.6% of validator dividends (chain, 2026-10-30).

What changes on 9 November

Team plan of 2026-10-23, confirmed 2026-10-29; starting values.

  • Testnet opens 2 November, mainnet starts 9 November, first results 12 November.
  • Entry: hotkeys scoring at least 0.75 on hidden known-answer cases in two or more recent review rounds.
  • Entrants submit private pipeline packages (open-weight models only, $0.09 per paper at most); only the team sees code, scores are public.
  • Pay: miner emission stays at 10%, split 70/20/10 among the top three; the team pays compute.

Getting started

  1. Read the README and agent miner README; use the reference agent miner or your own pipeline.
  2. Install Python 3.12 and Bittensor SDK 10.5.0; add a hosted-model key (OpenRouter is advised).
  3. Register a hotkey (0.05 TAO burn) and open a public TCP port.
  4. Answer every review task within 30 minutes and keep your last-three average at 0.75 or above.
  5. From 2026-11-02 try the contest on testnet; the first mainnet deadline is 2026-11-09 12:00 UTC.

Caveats

  • The 90% operator share is seen on chain; what the team does with it is its own statement.
  • Usage figures come from the team's own API; its endpoints disagree on totals, and a team post said 119 papers a day against 196 in the API.
  • Contest rules are starting values and may change; the rules file was due before 2 November.
  • Miners report flood attacks and selection bias; the team disputed both and neither was verified.
