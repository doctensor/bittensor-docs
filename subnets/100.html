What it is

Cortex describes itself as an autonomous research network. Miners compete in challenges run by the team, a master service seals each epoch's scores into one signed bundle, and validators check that bundle and set weights. Two challenges are in the code: Bounty, where miners file reproducible bug reports on Cortex products, and HyperTrain, a research-stage attempt at verifiable, permissionless pretraining of one shared language model. On 2026-10-21 the project's own site called Bounty the only challenge that pays and HyperTrain a preview with nothing paid. The subnet has carried three project names since its 2025 launch (Platform, then BASE, then Cortex), and the repositories warn that interfaces, wire formats and emission shares may change.

How it works

Miners

Bounty miners pair their hotkey with a dedicated Cortex Chat account, which the operator must authorise, and file reproducible bug reports; the status route sets limits of 5 pending reports per hotkey and 60 seconds between reports. HyperTrain miners register a hotkey, run the team's GPU image on the reference card (an RTX 5090), join a run, and train in rounds that other nodes can replay. The project's own guide says on 2026-10-21 that there is nothing to mine in HyperTrain yet, and its run table showed one participant, offline. The miner guide describes one GPU per process (last changed 2026-10-19), while the project README describes multi-GPU clusters.

Validators

A validator does not run challenges or rent GPUs. It downloads the sealed epoch bundle from the team's gateway, checks the hashes, Merkle root and challenge profile against the chain, recomputes the weights and submits them. Any registered hotkey can run one with a setup script from the repository. The master and the challenge containers run only on team infrastructure.

Incentive

Bounty gives one point per report that the team's public feed marks valid, whatever its severity, counted cumulatively with no reset (status route, 2026-10-21). The challenge pays its full share only once there are 10 valid reports across miners, and any shortfall burns. On 2026-10-21 the public board showed about 91,700 published reports, 99 percent of them valid, from 122 hotkeys, with about 17,000 still awaiting the team's review. HyperTrain rewards are meant to vest in escrow over about ten rounds and be forfeited if an auditor's replay finds a mismatch; the project says none has matured and its runs use a test ledger. Any challenge share that cannot be scored burns to uid 0. Chain read at block 9335130: the emission split is 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators and 41 percent miners, miner emission aimed at the owner's hotkey is burned, and uid 0 is the owner's own hotkey. From about 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-19 roughly 70 percent of incentive sat on uid 0 and the rest was spread over 26 to 85 other uids; since about 2026-10-20 all of it is on uid 0. A 2026-09-22 team status post gave the split as 30 percent Bounty and 70 percent Proof, with the Proof share burning, which fits that chain pattern; the repository files show 80/20, 70/30 and 35/35/30 splits in different places.

Getting started

  1. Read the cortex README and docs/CHALLENGES.md, then pick Bounty (no GPU, needs a dedicated Cortex Chat account); the project says HyperTrain is a preview with nothing to mine yet.
  2. Register a hotkey on subnet 100. HyperTrain admission (self-check, join, probation and bond steps in hypertrain/docs/miner.md) is for testing only until the team announces a live run.
  3. For Bounty, check the public status route: it must report that scoring is available (it said it could not score on 2026-10-21). Then ask the operator to authorise the account pairing and file reports.
  4. To validate, install libsodium and the cortex package, set the wallet environment variables, and run scripts/run-validator.sh with --verify-only first.
  5. Before spending money or hardware, read the network page, the bounty status route and the latest team posts in the subnet chat: on 2026-10-21 scoring was unavailable and all on-chain incentive went to the owner uid.

Caveats

  • Emission shares: the live split looks like 70/30 (team status post 2026-09-22 and the chain pattern 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-19, with the 70 percent slot burning), but repository files show 80/20 (development profile), 70/30 (one document) and 35/35/30 (template). The production profile is signed by the owner and was not read.
  • The deployment registry in the cortex repo (unchanged since 2026-10-20) lists the bounty and opentype containers but no hypertrain entry, and its profile still lists a proof challenge that the project's site calls retired, so how HyperTrain maps onto the 70 percent slot is not shown in code.
  • The team said on 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-12 that HyperTrain mining was live; on 2026-10-21 the project's page and guide call it a preview with no synced round and nothing paid, and a repository design note says no join is accepted. The guide calls the HyperTrain figures a simulated mock, while the page says they come from the challenge's public API: which is right was not settled. Treat HyperTrain as not paying until the team says otherwise.
  • A HyperTrain test-ledger run on the production challenge steers real emission as soon as its first reward matures (repo docs), and the repo gained production-issuance gating on 2026-10-21, so the switch from preview to paying could come with little notice.
  • The team said on 2026-10-05 that bounty rewards are spread over 24 hours, but the repository and the live status route on 2026-10-21 both describe cumulative scoring with no window. The guide's phrase 'precision times mean severity' matches neither and is probably stale. Why Bounty scoring is paused, and whether the 100 percent burn is deliberate, has not been explained by the team.
  • On 2026-10-21 six of the seven validators with a permit had set weights recently (median weight age about 311 blocks), all at 100 percent on uid 0; one validator's weights were about 100,000 blocks (about 14 days) old. A miner claim that weights were frozen from 2026-10-09 is not supported by chain history, where the number of uids with incentive grew from 27 to 86 between 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-18. Whether validators copy one vector was not tested.
  • Bounty reports are adjudicated by the team's operators with an AI agent, and the 99 percent valid rate on the public board is the team's own figure, not independently checked; a miner claim that a fabricated bug passed (2026-10-13) is unverified. Validators depend on a team-run gateway for the bundle, and the validator docs name a gateway domain that differs from the project website. Endpoints under /v1/site named in the guide, and two repositories it names (subnet-frontend and backend), returned 404 on 2026-10-21.
  • The HyperTrain evidence in the repository shows bitwise-identical training across hosts, but the changelog says matching centralized training quality is not yet shown. The miner guide (one GPU per process) and the README (multi-GPU e2e on 2x RTX 5090) disagree.
  • Not researched: scoring code traced end to end, the whitepaper, and product or ecosystem claims (apps, partnerships).
