What it is

It's AI is a subnet for detecting whether a text was written by a person or generated by a language model. Miners run classifiers and return a probability for each text in a batch; validators build the test batches themselves and score the answers. The team behind it, a company called ITSAI Technologies, also sells the detector as a website, API, browser extension and plugins, and says the subnet is the engine behind that product. Its benchmark and business figures are the team's own and were not independently checked.

How it works

Miners

A miner runs a text classifier on a GPU (the docs recommend at least an RTX A4000 with 16 GB, while the compute file says 24 GB) and returns a probability that each text in a batch is AI-written. The repo ships a perplexity-based baseline and a DeBERTa-large classifier, and the FAQ says the baseline would not survive on the live network. Since v3.15.0 (2026-07-24) predictions can be sealed so only the asking validator can read them, and validators currently accept both sealed and plain answers.

Validators

A validator builds each round's challenges itself: human texts from the Pile and AI texts generated by 30 or more open models through Ollama, in equal proportion, with augmentations such as misspellings and removed adjectives. A separate set drawn from Common Crawl is used as an out-of-domain test. Each round it queries a random sample of miners, and it re-asks a few texts in a smaller batch to see whether the answers match. The docs ask for an A100-class GPU with 80 GB of VRAM, 1 TB of disk, Ollama and a Weights and Biases key.

Incentive

Per the code, a miner's round reward is the average of three numbers on the in-domain texts: F1 score, a false-positive score (1 minus the share of wrongly flagged human texts) and average precision. The reward drops to zero if the miner's running out-of-domain F1 average (weight 0.2 on the newest round) is below 0.9, if answers fall outside 0 to 1, if the small-batch answers differ from the full-batch ones, or if the version check fails. Rewards are then passed through a softmax scaled by 100 across the sampled miners, and miners not sampled get zero for that round. Weights go on chain with commit-reveal. The docs describe only the three-part average, so they lag the code. Individual on-chain weights were not read.

Getting started

  1. Check the hardware first: the mining doc asks for a GPU of at least RTX A4000 class with 16 GB of VRAM, while min_compute.yml says 24 GB, and the FAQ says the baseline model will not pass the network's quality bar, so plan to bring your own classifier.
  2. Clone the repo, create a virtual environment and run python -m pip install -e . (confirm that the installed package is named detection). Download the DeBERTa classifier weights from the links in docs/mining.md into the models folder.
  3. Create a coldkey and hotkey and register with btcli s register --netuid 32. No alpha holding is needed to mine since v3.14, but members describe registration as competitive with all slots filled, so check the current burn first (0.0128 TAO at the 2026-10-21 read).
  4. Start the miner with neurons/miner.py under pm2, with a reachable axon port and your GPU device. The docs advise a local Subtensor node because the public endpoint was under heavy load.
  5. To test before spending, generate data the way validators do with detection/data_generator.py and score your model locally. The out-of-domain F1 must stay at or above 0.9 on average. The docs name testnet netuid 87 for trials, but a member said on 2026-09-28 that no testnet validators were sending challenges, and this was not checked.
  6. Validators need an A100-class GPU with 80 GB of VRAM, about 1 TB of disk, Ollama running under systemd and a Weights and Biases key, per docs/validating.md.

Caveats

  • The repo has had no commits since 2026-07-23 (version 3.15.0, step 1 of a two-step move to encrypted predictions; the second step is not in the repo), and the last team update post is dated 2026-07-24. The team said on 2026-08-12 it is not changing direction. Whether work continues privately was not established.
  • Accuracy, revenue and traffic figures (a number-one ranking on one public benchmark, about 99 percent accuracy, a false-positive rate under 1 percent, visit counts and market sizing) come from the team's README and site. They were not independently verified, and the site's accuracy page was not read.
  • Docs and chat conflict in several places. A pinned chat post from 2025-03-10 announced a 500-alpha minimum for miners, but the team removed it on 2026-03-10; the code option still exists with a default of 0, and whether any validator sets it higher was not checked. The incentive doc omits the out-of-domain gate and the softmax step. The roadmap doc still describes two generator models. Miner VRAM is 16 GB in one file and 24 GB in another.
  • Validator concentration: the chain shows dividends going to 4 UIDs with about 85 percent to one of them, and a member reported on 2026-10-20 that a validator holding most of the stake had stalled for hours. The team said it was resolved; the cause was not stated. One permit holder has apparently never set weights in over 6.7 million blocks. Only chain aggregates were read, not individual weights.
  • Operational reports in the last month include an incentive stall (2026-10-02), validator logging to Weights and Biases stopping (2026-10-16) and another stall (2026-10-20). Members also described a registration contest and thin miner margins; those are opinions and were not checked. No price, emission or market figures are given on this page.
  • The Weights and Biases page is client-rendered and was not read, and the testnet netuid 87 named in the docs was not checked on chain.
