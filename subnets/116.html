What it is

SN116 is run by Carbon Physics, Inc., which describes the subnet as discovery and evidence infrastructure for physics AI. Researchers and AI agents compete to find better ways of building fast models that stand in for slow physics simulations (battery charging, chip cooling, electric motors and similar). Carbon's validators rebuild each candidate themselves and test it against reference physics before it can count. The slot was registered on mainnet on 2026-10-04 and the team says mining has not opened; on chain all validator weight currently goes to the owner's uid 0. The company's plan is to sell independent model evaluations and research to engineering customers.

How it works

Miners

Miners do not submit trained weights. They submit a reproducible construction recipe: the backbone, training procedure, hyperparameters, data handling, seeds and resource budget needed to rebuild the model from scratch. The website names Fourier Neural Operators and DeepONets as the launch families, and a 2026-10-17 repo design note says any complete, explicit pinned recipe should be allowed in time, with pretrained checkpoints barred. The owner said on 2026-10-22 that a miner may enter whichever challenge it likes, and that each challenge has its own practice image for miners and its own evaluation pack for validators. Miners practise on public practice cases and never see the protected exam cases. Carbon supplies a local browser app (Launchpad / Miner Control Center), a research agent called Graphite and an MCP interface for outside agents. As of 2026-10-21 submitting on testnet was not open: miners download the tools and practise locally.

Validators

Per the website and repo, a validator rebuilds the recipe on its own data in a fresh worker with no network, freezes the result as an inert model and tests it in a separate runtime. Mandatory physics checks come first (for example conservation, boundary behaviour and stability); failing one makes a result inadmissible whatever its accuracy. In the public scoring code (commit of 2026-10-18) a score pack has hard gates and three legs, physics, robustness and accuracy, combined as a weighted geometric mean, so a zero in any leg gives zero overall; the public packs are development fixtures. The owner said on 2026-10-22 that each score will be half accuracy and physics behaviour and half design value (how well the model picks the best design for each condition), and on 2026-10-16 that validation includes simulated design decisions made against the solver. That design-value half is not in the public score pack: its definition in the repo (value metric v2) is a development draft whose values the owner has yet to set.

Incentive

Not paying. On chain (mainnet, block 9355283, 2026-10-24 18:36Z) the subnet receives emission, split 18% owner, 41% validators and 41% miners, and RecycleOrBurn is set to Burn. All four validators with a permit give 100% of their weight to uid 0, the owner's hotkey, so uid 0 holds 100% of the incentive (burned on the miner side) and, with its validator dividends, about 82% of what uids earned in the last epoch. No other miner uid has incentive; the same picture was read on 2026-10-23. The repo's rule (carbon/rewards/winner_decay.py) is written as a testnet policy for netuid 567 with three challenges and no mainnet policy file was found: an epoch is split equally over the registered challenges, a challenge's current winner gets its full share for 24 hours, the share then halves every further 24 hours, and everything unpaid burns to uid 0. The owner said on 2026-10-22 that emissions will be split evenly between active challenges. An older reward contract in the repo runs only in a local fixture. The code that would publish winner weights is an owner-run publisher that needs a standing owner authorization, publishes at most once per 360-block epoch and refuses an owner-coldkey winner on mainnet; the public repo holds no mainnet authorization or policy, and its chain constants still name testnet 567 only. The owner said on 2026-10-10 that there are no emissions until they are turned on, and on 2026-10-19 that miners will not be paid until the system produces something a customer will pay for.

Getting started

  1. Mining has not opened and nothing is paid, so this is for trying the development tools only.
  2. Clone the GitHub repo and open it in the Ubuntu 24.04 dev container the repo provides (CPU is the supported profile).
  3. Run ./scripts/dev/bootstrap.sh, then ./scripts/dev/doctor.sh to check the environment matches what validators use.
  4. Run ./scripts/dev/canonical.sh --full to run a versioned example.
  5. To use the research tools (Launchpad, Graphite or the MCP interface) a miner must be registered on the subnet; the testnet used for development is netuid 567 (registered 2026-09-15). The owner said on 2026-10-21 that submitting on testnet is not open yet, so practise locally. A development submit is a signed message to a local service and writes nothing to the chain.
  6. Watch the #116 channel: the owner said he will announce there first when the tools are fully tested. As of 2026-10-24 the practice-flow fix he described on 2026-10-20 was not on the repo's public main branch.

Caveats

  • The subnet is not paying miners and the team gave no launch date: 4-6 weeks (2026-10-06), about a week or two (2026-10-13) and, on 2026-10-21, that it is coming with a clean four-challenge version aimed for that week have all been stated, and nothing had launched by 2026-10-24.
  • The launch set is unsettled. The website portfolio (unchanged between 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-24) lists eight proposed challenges; the owner said 3 were wired in (2026-10-06), aimed for 4 (2026-10-21) and named 5, not promised and still being qualified (2026-10-22). The repo's only registered weight policy lists three testnet challenges.
  • Chain read: at block 9355283 (2026-10-24) all four validators with a permit give 100% to uid 0, the owner's hotkey and the burn uid in the code. Of the three other permit holders, two are hotkeys registered on 120 and 82 subnets (general multi-subnet validators, not Carbon-specific) and one is registered only on this subnet with almost no stake of its own; why they match the owner, and which software they run, was not checked. The chain's emission flag is on, which only means the subnet receives emission.
  • Reward rules: the 24-hour winner-decay rule is code for a testnet policy only (netuid 567, three challenges, last changed 2026-10-14); no mainnet policy file was found in the public repo, and the publisher code is owner-run, needs a standing authorization and refuses an owner-coldkey winner on mainnet, but the repo holds no mainnet authorization, so who sets the live mainnet weights is not shown by the repo. The older SCORE_REWARDS contract runs only in a local fixture.
  • The owner's description of the score (half accuracy and physics, half design value) exists only in chat. The public score pack has three legs (physics, robustness, accuracy), its packs are fixtures, and the design-value metric is a development draft.
  • Testnet 567 is not a live competition: at block 8293532 (2026-10-24) it had 31 neurons, emission off, no incentive and validator weights at least 11 days old.
  • The repo's own status guide (reviewed 2026-09-28) still lists four challenges; the readiness record (2026-10-14) and website list eight, with readiness records for four. Every readiness record is a proposed development design and nothing is described as scientifically or production qualified.
  • The repo does not establish any signed paying customers, validated prices or proven demand; the commercial plan (evidence audits, sponsored research, Alpha buyback and burn) is intent, not a recorded result.
  • Most of the repo (about 8,000 files) was read at documentation level; only the reward policy, winner-decay, score-combine and weight-publisher code were read in code. The owner's weekly X updates, a TAO Daily post and a revenue-show episode on the commercial plan were not readable.
  • The repo licence is MIT for subnet software but proprietary for the Business, website and presentations folders, and it grants no rights in methods miners submit. GitHub shows the licence as unrecognised.
  • The earlier chat history in this channel (July to early October) is about a previous occupant of netuid 116 and should not be read as Carbon's history.
