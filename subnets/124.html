What it is

Swarm is an open benchmark for drone-flying AI, run by a team building a physical drone lab. Miners train flight models in simulation and submit them privately; validators fly them through generated worlds. Since 2026-11-10 one mission, Sentinel, holds all emission.

How it works

Miners

A miner builds a flight policy and submits it with the swarm command-line tool, which commits its hash on chain and uploads the model to the team's private vault. One hotkey gets one model, in one mission, for good. Models queue, then run the full benchmark (2,000 seeds in Sentinel).

Validators

Validators claim seeds for one model at a time from a shared pool on the team's backend, fly them in Docker and send scores back. The backend decides the champion list; each validator recomputes weights from it with the open formula. Only whitelisted coldkeys get evaluation work (runbook); the others mirror weights.

Incentive

Each mission's share pays its last five champions (ten in Sentinel), at 0.7 to the power of rank plus up to 30% for how much of the gap to a perfect score they closed. Chain split since 2026-11-10: 18% owner, 49% validators, 33% miners (41% each before).

Swarm Sentinel (live since 2026-10-29)

  • A drone patrols a simulated solar park day and night, reports every intruder and lands back in its dock.
  • Seed score: 70% detection, 20% coverage, 10% flight; each miss or false alarm zeroes 5 of a model's best seeds.
  • Twelve champions are crowned and the last ten are paid (best score 0.981, UID 138); their models stay private. A screening phase exists in code but is off.
  • Champions cash out: UID 91's stake was emptied three times in two days.

Getting started

  1. Read the miner guide and the Sentinel guide.
  2. Install the repository on Ubuntu (Python 3.11); copy your mission's starter agent.
  3. Test locally with swarm model test; epoch seeds are secret.
  4. Register a fresh hotkey on subnet 124 (0.5 TAO burn).
  5. Run swarm model submit. The slot locks after evaluation; expect a queue.
  6. Validators: ask the team for backend access; an approved coldkey is also required.

Caveats

  • The chain shows what is paid, not that scoring is fair: the champion list comes from a closed backend.
  • About 44% of emission goes to the owner side (18% cut plus its validator's dividends; our arithmetic). The split moved toward validators on 2026-11-10 (chain event decoded); who set it is unread.
  • Queue speed and compute figures are the team's words (last read 2026-10-31); the feed no longer shows upload or score times.
  • UID 0 is the burn address in code but the owner's own slot on chain; burned weight is untested.
  • Revenue, funding and client claims are the team's own. The Q&A bot is not team policy.
