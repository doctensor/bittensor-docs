What it is

Swarm is an open benchmark for the AI that flies drones, run by a team that is also building a physical drone lab. Miners train a flight model in simulation for one mission, submit it privately, and validators fly it through procedurally generated worlds; each mission has its own champion lineage and its own fixed share of emissions. Winning models are published as open source after they take a crown. The team says the winning models feed its commercial drone work and that it has two signed pilot contracts; those figures are the team's own and were not verified.

How it works

Miners

A miner builds a flight policy for one mission and packages it as a zip with a drone_agent.py file that holds a DroneFlightController class, plus a policy contract file. The swarm command-line tool (swarm model submit) checks the archive locally, commits its SHA-256 and the mission on chain from the miner's hotkey, and uploads the archive, signed by that hotkey, to the team's backend, which keeps it in a private vault. A hotkey gets one model in one mission for good: once the model is evaluated the slot is locked, even if it failed, and a new attempt needs a new hotkey. Archives that duplicate an existing one are refused, with the earliest chain commit winning, and no new commitments are taken in the last 1.5 hours of an epoch. A new model goes straight to the full 1,000-seed benchmark (a 300-seed screening step exists in the code but is switched off); the two latest crowned models were scored about one hour and about two hours twenty minutes after upload. Models that win a crown are published within minutes to the open swarm-champions repository and the leaderboard page; models that never win stay private. A GPU is not required by the repository's compute file, and the local benchmark uses the same simulator and scoring path as validators, though the epoch's own seeds stay secret until it closes.

Validators

Validators run the evaluation in Docker containers on a 12-core, 48 GB Linux host, claim seeds from the team's backend, fly each model through its mission and send per-seed scores back. The backend address and the logging key are issued by the team. The repository's operator runbook (which describes backend code that is not public) says evaluation tasks go only to validators whose coldkey is on a team-kept whitelist and that meet a minimum stake (default 4,096); other validators only mirror weights. The chain agrees with that picture: at block 9343854 (2026-10-23) eleven neurons held validator permits, ten had set weights and all ten rows were identical (distance 0.0), the owner's own validator held 75.6 percent of validator stake and one other held 22.5 percent, and one permit holder had set no weights for about 27 days. The mission list, allocations, solve thresholds, bans and the champion lineage are kept in the team's backend. Each validator does recompute the weights itself from the backend's list of kings using the open formula in the repository, drops any king whose hotkey no longer matches the metagraph, and sends unpaid shares to UID 0. Epochs are 14 days (they were 7 days before epoch 19, per the code constants; epoch 25 starts on 2026-10-26 at 16:00 UTC by that arithmetic) and each brings 1,000 fresh seeds per mission; champions are re-scored on them but keep their crown even if the new score is lower, and a challenger must beat the champion on the same epoch's seeds by a margin.

Incentive

Emissions are split first by mission, with fixed shares set by the team: Office Interceptor 30 percent, Swarm Autopilot 20, Swarm Search and Rescue 20, Autopilot 15 and Search and Rescue 15. The first mission, Interceptor, was solved on 2026-08-24 and now pays 0. Inside a mission, the last five champions are paid. Each seat's weight is 0.7 to the power of its rank times a bonus of up to 30 percent for how much of the remaining distance to a perfect score the crowning closed, and the seats are then normalised. To take the crown a challenger must beat the champion by a floor that shrinks from 0.015 to 0.005 as the champion's score rises. A mission with no eligible king or an archived mission pays nothing, and its share goes to UID 0. A seed's score is 45 percent success, 45 percent time and 10 percent safety (by default clearance of 1 m earns the full safety term and 0.2 m none; some terrain types have their own distances); the interceptor missions use 50 percent caught and 50 percent time; most failed flights that took off score 0.01 rather than 0 and an evaluation error scores 0. Check against the chain: at block 9343854 (2026-10-23) the 25 paid kings, weighted by the public kings feed and the mission shares, equalled the incentive on chain for all 25 UIDs to 0.00 percentage points, no other UID had incentive and none went to UID 0; the biggest single share was 10.1 percent. Reigning champions had crowning scores of about 0.990 in Autopilot, 0.985 in Search and Rescue, 0.856 in Swarm Autopilot, 0.990 in Swarm Search and Rescue and 0.996 in Office Interceptor. The chain's emission split for the subnet reads 18 percent owner, 41 validators and 41 miners; within the miner share, the above decides who gets what.

Getting started

  1. Read the miner guide and the family guide for the one mission you want (docs/families in the repository). One hotkey enters one mission with one model, so pick before registering.
  2. Install the repository on Ubuntu with the miner setup scripts (Python 3.11), then copy the template agent for your mission into your own folder; Office Interceptor has its own starter file.
  3. Test locally with the swarm command-line tool, passing the mission id; the local benchmark uses the same simulator and scoring as validators, but the epoch's seeds are secret, so a local score is a guide only.
  4. Create a coldkey and a fresh hotkey and register the hotkey on subnet 124 (the registration burn read 0.5 TAO, the chain minimum, on 2026-10-23). Treat the submission as final: the slot locks once the model is evaluated.
  5. Run swarm model submit with the source folder, the mission id and the wallet names. It packages, checks, commits the digest on chain and uploads to the team's backend. It refuses in the last 1.5 hours of an epoch (epochs are 14 days; the next starts 2026-10-26 at 16:00 UTC by the code's schedule).
  6. Expect a full 1,000-seed run for the new model; the last two crowned models were scored between one and two and a half hours after upload. Check the live leaderboard; if your model is crowned it is published to the champions repository, and you can study past champions there before building the next model.
  7. Validators: the guide says to ask the team in Discord for the backend address and a logging key, and the operator runbook says evaluation needs the team to add your coldkey to its whitelist.

Caveats

  • The chain proves what is paid, not that the evaluation is fair: the payout list comes from the team's backend and matched the chain exactly on 2026-10-23, but the backend (queue, model vault, champion lineage, bans, mission shares, solve thresholds) is closed source, so seeds, scores and bans cannot be checked in code.
  • Scoring is team-controlled in practice: validators need the team's backend address and, per the repository's runbook, a whitelisted coldkey. On chain the owner's own validator held 75.6 percent of validator stake on 2026-10-23, and the other nine weight-setting validators submitted the same vector, so they mirror it.
  • Swarm Sentinel is a design, not a live mission. The unmerged branch documents 250 seeds per epoch, a rule that zeroes ten of a model's best seeds for every miss or false alarm, a ten-king window and a 0.65 share. The branch docs also say that share burns until the mission has its first king; if deployed as written, 65 percent of emissions could burn at launch. The team said the branch could still change, and it had not moved since 2026-10-13.
  • Office Interceptor was called solved by the team on 2026-10-07, yet it still pays: the public API lists it as active and on 2026-10-23 its five kings held 30.0 percent of incentive on chain (champion re-score about 0.994). The solve threshold is secret, so when or whether it archives is unclear.
  • The code calls UID 0 a burn address, but on chain UID 0 is the subnet owner's own hotkey slot. Whether weight sent there is destroyed or reaches the owner was not checked, and no weight went to UID 0 on 2026-10-23, so it is untested in practice.
  • One validator-permit holder had set no weights for about 27 days on 2026-10-23; the reason is unknown.
  • Docs disagree with each other: the Office Interceptor guide still says active and public while all missions are private; the template README in the champions repository mentions 1,100 seeds, weekly rotation and a 0.02 floor for Search and Rescue where the main README says 1,000 seeds, 14-day epochs and a 0.015 to 0.005 floor; the main README badge shows version 5.1.5.0 and the changelog stops at 5.1.6.1 while the code is 5.1.6.5.
  • Revenue, the investor round, the lab and the statement that very little owner alpha has been sold are the team's own claims and were not verified; the CEO said on 2026-10-03 that buybacks have not started. The team newsletter has not posted since 2026-04-27, so it is not a current source.
  • The ban on 2026-09-28 and the claim about a shared owner behind two hotkeys were not resolved publicly.
  • A community Q&A bot answers in the channel on the team's behalf; its answers are not team policy. A miner question about a weekly no-progress burn has no matching rule in the current docs or code.
