What it is

MVTRX (earlier called tau-os, repository name sn-79) runs many simulated limit-order-book markets in which miners operate as trading agents against a population of background agents. The stated aim is to reward risk-aware trading and real liquidity and to produce detailed market data for research. The same team also builds a separate off-chain exchange for alpha tokens, a training pool for an order-book generative model (GenTRX), and a set of trading tools; per the team's own roadmap, the exchange currently runs only on a localnet with test funds. On chain, a single validator, the owner's, sets every miner's weight.

How it works

Miners

A miner runs a trading agent, a Python class that returns orders each time a validator sends a state update for the simulated books. The example agents in the repository are described by the team as not expected to win, so a miner needs its own strategy. Replies must arrive within the validator's timeout (3 seconds by default), and response time sets how much simulated delay the miner's orders get; until 0.6.5 the delay depended slightly on UID order (about 4 ms between low and high UIDs, confirmed by the team on 2026-10-20), and the 0.6.5 code on the test branch sends each round's requests in a fresh random order. At most five instructions per book are accepted per round. Each UID also has a rolling volume cap per book of 10 times its starting capital over 24 simulated hours (500,000 per book on the live run); hitting it blocks new orders on that book, and the cap carries over between simulation runs. Since 0.6.4 every miner receives each book's fundamental price in its state update. Miners can optionally also train the shared GenTRX model by uploading compressed gradients to their own S3 bucket. Turnover is high: on 2026-10-23, 61 of the 256 UIDs had been registered in the previous day and the median UID was 3 days old.

Validators

Validators host the C++ simulator next to a Python validator that sends state to miners, feeds their orders into the simulation, calculates scores and sets weights from a moving average. Each validator runs its own stochastic simulation of the same configuration, so results differ between validators; the FAQ gives this as one cause of vTrust gaps. The owner designs and publishes the simulation parameters and, per the README, intends a new configuration roughly weekly; the team's dashboard showed new runs opening on 2026-10-10, 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-19. The recommended validator host is 16 cores, 32 GB RAM, Ubuntu 22.04 or later and g++ 14. Validators may opt in to GenTRX, which needs a GPU for a gradient server. On chain on 2026-10-23 only one UID held a validator permit, and it belongs to the subnet owner (its hotkey and coldkey match the owner's); the hotkey held about 61 percent of the subnet's alpha. That validator sets all the weights, with commit-reveal on, so no other validator currently influences miner incentive, and the team's dashboard publishes metrics from the same single validator.

Incentive

Miner pay comes from two pools. About 95 percent goes to trading and up to 5 percent to GenTRX training, scaled by how many miners take part (an unused training share returns to trading). The trading pool is split into two equal halves. The making half pays for liquidity: spread captured on maker fills, counted only on books where the miner fills on both sides, scaled by how much of that spread the fill still holds 20 simulated seconds later, discounted when fills come from a few counterparties, limited to 4 times the miner's fills against the background market when it trades mostly with other miners, and since 0.6.4 net of a small weighted share of the maker fee (weight 0.005 at launch, to be raised later). The skill half pays for trading profit after the market's drift on held inventory is removed; a book counts only if its profit clears 2.3 basis points of the miner's own filled notional, and a miner qualifies with consistent positive profit on at least about 15.6 percent of the books (15 of 96 and 5 of 32 on the live two-class layout). Since 2026-09-28 both halves are paid in proportion to measured contribution rather than by rank, so splitting one strategy over many UIDs should not earn more. Since 0.6.4 the two asset classes are weighted by their share of the books (75 and 25 percent). The 0.6.5 code on the test branch changes the layout to three classes (64, 32 and 32 books) weighted 50, 25 and 25 percent, with skill bars of 10 of 64, 5 of 32 and 5 of 32; it also publishes extra per-miner statistics (liquidity provided, a second position valuation) that are explicitly not paid. Scores are smoothed over a track record of about three simulated hours. On chain the emission split is 18 percent owner, 41 percent validators and 41 percent miners, with nothing burned or recycled, and on 2026-10-23 the miner incentive vector had 245 non-zero UIDs, the top miner at 1.8 percent, the top 10 at about 11 percent and the top 50 at about 36 percent.

No data was shown with the claim, and the archived preview of the post was cut off.
  • 2026-10-21 (#79-mvtrx, GitHub) 0.6.5 went to testnet. The team said mainnet would follow on Friday 2026-10-23 and that the third asset class would join at the first new simulation run after that, expected over the weekend. The release adds that third class (priced like the first, on a finer price grid; pool split 50, 25 and 25 percent by share of books), a fresh random query order each round, margin calls that keep working until a loan is settled, a making credit that waits for the full trading picture around a fill on quiet books, and extra per-miner statistics that are published but not paid. Agents built for 0.6.4 are said to run unchanged. The public repository's test branch carries the code (version 0.6.5, 2026-10-21); main was still 0.6.4.
  • 2026-10-20 (#79-mvtrx) The team confirmed that response-time delay depends on UID order, because requests are prepared in UID order while the validator clock for each miner has already started (about 4 ms of simulated delay between low and high UIDs). The next validator update will randomise query order and a later release will fix the underlying delays. It said 0.6.5 would reach testnet later that week and mainnet after a few days of observation, with 0.6.6 after that. Asked about makers and takers, it said the 50/50 split between the making and skill halves is intended and fixed, that it will not fix roles per hotkey, and that the weight between the halves is a dial it can move.
  • 2026-10-16 (#79-mvtrx, GitHub) 0.6.4 went to mainnet during the running simulation, so balances and positions carried on: making credit became net of a small share of the maker fee, the second asset class rose from 5 to 25 percent of the pool, and every book's fundamental price now arrives in each miner's state. A status update the same day announced 0.6.5 with a third asset class for the following week and said TradingView charts and anonymous access were ready but waiting on final confirmations.
  • 2026-10-14 (#79-mvtrx, GitHub) A miner disclosed that the fundamental-price seed can be reconstructed from public BTC price data. The code in the repository does derive the seed from the rounded BTC price plus the book number. The team said it tested 60 steps against every seed in a 20,000-price window and none matched, and that seed generation would be hardened in an upcoming release; it asked for a working reconstruction to check. The same day 0.6.4 went to testnet.
  • 2026-10-09 (#79-mvtrx, GitHub) 0.6.3 reached mainnet in two steps. On 2026-10-09 the validator update capped making credit from fills against other miners at four times fills against the background market. Around 2026-10-10 a new run opened with a second asset class on books 96 to 127 (5 percent of the pool at first) and a new making score that judges each fill by how much spread it still holds 20 seconds later; balances and positions reset, scores carried over.
  • 2026-10-06 (#79-mvtrx) Miners reported about 60 registrations a day and a high registration fee. The team said the UIDs that rotated out over the previous day had earned between 0.6 and 1.1 times the registration fee they paid, and that 0.6.3, on testnet that day, would address the point; that release also added a second simulated asset class.
  • 2026-09-28 (#79-mvtrx) Proportional pay went live on mainnet: both halves are paid by share of measured contribution instead of rank, and each account's skill bar became 2.3 basis points of its own filled notional per book. On 2026-09-29 the team said the largest coordinated account set held 9.2 percent of validator weight and 7.9 percent of on-chain incentive, down several-fold, and that 7 percent of its maker fills came from the open market against 77 percent for all other makers. It made no further change and said it would watch longer; on 2026-10-01 it said that set was still paid for some flow it supplies to itself and that further measures were being evaluated. These are the team's own measurements.
  • 2026-09-23 (#79-mvtrx) After miners reported wash trading (large sets of paid accounts supported by money-losing counterparty accounts), the team confirmed one reported set, saying most of its fills came from a handful of accounts that lose on every fill, and said a second reported set was not confirmed. It declined to ban by IP address or coldkey, arguing both are cheap to change, and promised scoring fixes in 0.6.2. 0.6.2 went to testnet that day and to mainnet on 2026-09-24: the first market configuration chosen by automated calibration (a search over about 800 candidate markets scored against real exchange data), skill counted only for accounts trading many books, and the counterparty discount extended to the skill half.

Getting started

  1. Read the FAQ and the README sections on the incentive mechanism, then the agent guide; the scoring rules change between releases, so check the FAQ against the latest announcement.
  2. Install the miner on Ubuntu with install_miner.sh; example agents are copied to a local folder, but the team says they are not expected to perform, so write your own strategy.
  3. Test locally with the proxy validator tools described in the agents folder, which run a local simulation engine against the background market.
  4. Optional: register on testnet netuid 366 and watch your miner on the testnet dashboard. The team said on 2026-10-08 it can send a small amount of testnet TAO to miners registered on mainnet; otherwise use the usual Bittensor Discord testnet faucet channel.
  5. Register on mainnet netuid 79 (the registration burn was about 0.08 TAO on 2026-10-23, and about 60 UIDs a day turn over) and run the miner. Keep replies under the validator timeout, plan around the per-book volume cap and the five-instruction limit, and expect a new miner's score to build over about three simulated hours.
  6. After each announcement, pull the published tree and restart if the release needs it. 0.6.5 is announced for mainnet on 2026-10-23 with a third asset class at the next run: read each book's class, price grid and minimum order from the state rather than from fixed book ranges (the repository's agents/MIGRATION_0.6.3.md covers the multi-class layout). The team targets Wednesday or Friday releases, testnet first, and says most releases need no code change from miners.
  7. Validators: host the C++ simulator and the Python validator (16 cores, 32 GB, Ubuntu 22.04 or later, g++ 14); GenTRX is optional and needs a GPU. Only run code from the official repository, since the team and moderators warn that impersonators send unsolicited direct messages.

Caveats

  • On chain on 2026-10-23 (block 9343607) only one UID held a validator permit, and its hotkey and coldkey match the subnet owner's. That validator sets all the weights, so miner incentive on chain is one team-run validator's opinion of the simulation; the team's dashboard matches the chain vector exactly, so it is a faithful mirror, but there is no independent validator to check it against. The hotkey held about 61 percent of the subnet's alpha (delegated stake is not necessarily the owner's own). Weights are validator opinions, not payouts. The dashboard lists 257 agents against 256 UIDs on chain, not explained.
  • The pay rules were revised in 0.6.2 (2026-09-24), 0.6.3 (2026-10-09 and 10-10) and 0.6.4 (2026-10-16), and 0.6.5 (announced for mainnet on 2026-10-23, with 0.6.6 after) changes the asset-class layout and weights again. The README on main still says mainnet runs the first asset class on all 128 books until the second arrives (it arrived on 2026-10-10) and describes a roughly weekly configuration, while live run ids showed starts on 10-10, 10-14 and 10-19; the 0.6.5 README on the test branch is corrected for three classes. The FAQ and the config help text in the code were the most current sources. Statements here about 0.6.5 describe code on a test branch and the team's announcement, not what mainnet runs.
  • The exchange is not live on mainnet. The README calls the MVTRX exchange live and the project's marketing site describes an alpha-token exchange, but the team's roadmap lists the market as running on a localnet with test funds, and marks the move to a mainnet market with real capital and incentives as gated, more than three months out. The README says netuid 79 declares a single mechanism today, which the chain confirms.
  • Wash trading and account farming were a major dispute in late September and early October. The team's published numbers on the largest coordinated set are its own measurements and were not independently checked, and some miners said coordinated flow was still being paid on 2026-10-01. The team says it will not ban by IP or coldkey and relies on scoring changes. On 2026-10-23 the chain showed the 256 UIDs under only 37 coldkeys, the largest holding 30 UIDs (13.9 percent of incentive), the next 25 UIDs (11.9 percent) and 19 UIDs (7.0 percent), and 18 coldkeys with five or more UIDs holding 220 UIDs and 88 percent of incentive. A shared coldkey does not prove a single operator or strategy (hosting services and teams can share one, and the team lists managed miner hosting), and operators can use several coldkeys, so this is a floor on operator concentration, not a finding of wash trading; per-account fills and counterparties were not read.
  • Fairness issues acknowledged or raised in chat: the UID-order delay effect confirmed on 2026-10-20 is fixed in the 0.6.5 test-branch code (fresh random query order each round) but was not on mainnet at 04:25 UTC on 2026-10-23. For the fundamental-price seed described on 2026-10-14, the 0.6.5 code now mixes the price and book number through a hash before seeding, but the input is still the last price from a public exchange price feed. The team disputes that the old seed could be reconstructed and I did not test either claim.
  • The team controls the simulation configuration, scoring parameters and release cadence, and the pay rule is a team-set formula. Several scoring dials (such as the maker-fee weight and the balance between the two halves) are described by the team as adjustable, so rewards can shift between releases, and the team's claim on 2026-10-22 that liquidity doubled and aggressive volume fell by three quarters since 2026-09-28 came without data.
  • Whether mining is profitable was not assessed; a newcomer who asked on 2026-10-18 had no answer in the channel. Registration turnover is high: 61 UIDs registered in the 24 hours before 2026-10-23, 185 within 7 days, and only about 5 of 256 UIDs were older than 30 days, with a registration burn of about 0.08 TAO. The team's own figure for UIDs that rotated out over one day in early October was a return of 0.6 to 1.1 times the registration fee.
  • Statements about conferences, research collaborations, investor talks, hiring and cross-chain plans come from the team and its roadmap page and were not verified. The roadmap lists a buyback programme as planned for one to three months out; none had started as far as the page shows.
  • Impersonation: moderators warned on 2026-10-13 that scammers send direct messages in the team's name and that neither the team nor moderators will message or friend-request members first.
