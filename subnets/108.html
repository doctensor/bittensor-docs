What it is

ChipForge asks miners to design real digital hardware, written in Verilog or SystemVerilog, and rewards designs that beat the best score so far. The first mainnet contest, Quest 001 (2026-10-06 to 2026-10-15), asked for a small neural processing unit (NPU) plus software kernels that run four edge-AI models exactly, scored on silicon area and speed (power was not scored). The team behind it is Tatsu. It says it plans to publish winning designs as open source and move toward fabricated chips, and its site lists an open RISC-V microcontroller from earlier competitions. The subnet was registered on mainnet on 2026-09-20 as netuid 108.

How it works

Miners

Miners download a challenge package with the specification and evaluator through the project's command-line tool, design and test locally, and submit a zip of Verilog or SystemVerilog files signed with their hotkey. Each hotkey gets a limited number of submissions per challenge (5 by default) and can see its own full evaluation logs after each batch closes. Winning designs are revealed to registered hotkeys after a delay, 18 hours on the live setting, or when a newer winner overtakes them.

Validators

Validators run a ChipForge evaluation server on their own machine (at least 16 physical CPU cores and 32 GB of memory) that simulates and synthesizes each design and returns results for function, area and speed. They need a secret key from the team and a validator permit on the subnet. A team-run challenge server groups submissions into batches, collects every validator's scores when a batch closes and decides the winner. Validators then compute payout places themselves from the schedule that server sends. On 2026-10-21 and again on 2026-10-23 the chain showed five validators with permits, all setting weights; on 2026-10-21 they sat on five separate coldkeys. Quest 001 scoring, read from its public package on 2026-10-23: a design must reproduce all four models' outputs bit for bit and make the system at least 2 times faster than a no-accelerator baseline (a clock above the baseline's 62.89 MHz does not count). It is then scored as throughput gained, to the power 0.7, divided by the NPU's synthesized area, to the power 0.3, with area floored at 1 square millimetre. Power is not scored. Each quest's package carries its own scoring settings, so Quest 2 may differ.

Incentive

On the live rules (checked 2026-10-23, unchanged since 2026-10-20) a submission becomes a winner record if it passes the correctness gates and beats the score to beat by more than a margin, now 5 percent after starting at 20 percent. A record waits out a 24-hour probation, then is paid for 24 hours; up to three records share the payout in a 1 to 0.35 to 0.12 ratio. Only 10 percent of each validator's weight is meant for miners. The other 90 percent goes to UID 0, which the team calls the burn UID (on chain it is the subnet owner's own slot), and everything goes there while no record is being paid. This ladder reached the public validator code on 2026-10-17, after Quest 001 closed: the team's API shows Quest 001's last winner was paid from the moment it qualified, for a flat 24 hours, with no probation. The team's challenge server also sends every validator the miner percentage, the ladder and any emission ban, and the validator code uses the server's miner percentage instead of its own setting, so one team-run server decides what all validators pay. The chain's own emission split was still the default on 2026-10-23 (owner 18, validators 41, miners 41 percent); the chain showed no sign that the new owner-set split was in use.

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 108 (the registration burn was at the 0.05 TAO minimum at block 9342911, 2026-10-23) and clone the ChipForge_SN108 repository; the README covers installation, with Docker recommended and Bittensor 10.x pinned.
  2. Check the public snapshot endpoint to see whether a challenge is active. On 2026-10-23 none was; the team expects Quest 2 between 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-26. When one is active, run the miner command-line tool's download command to get the specification and evaluator. The archived Quest 001 package, with its evaluator and scoring settings, can be downloaded without signing to see what a quest involves.
  3. Design in Verilog or SystemVerilog, score locally with the evaluator in the package, and use the tool's dry-run option to check your zip.
  4. Submit with the miner tool (zip up to 50 MB) and follow results on the leaderboard; full logs for your own submission arrive once its batch closes. For payout rules use the live rules endpoint, not the white paper.

Caveats

  • Between contests nothing is paid: the team's snapshot and chain reads on 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-23 both show 100 percent burn since 2026-10-16. The date for Quest 2 is a team estimate (7 to 10 days on 2026-10-16, weekend or Monday on 2026-10-21).
  • Only 10 percent of weight is meant for miners, with the rest sent to UID 0 according to the live rules and repository defaults. On chain UID 0 is the subnet owner's own hotkey; whether the protocol itself destroys alpha earned by the owner's hotkey was not checked. On 2026-10-23 UID 0 received about 80 percent of the subnet's last-epoch alpha emission.
  • Documentation and live values differ. The README example uses a 0.6 decay between payout places while live rules show 0.35, and the example environment file defaults to a 20 percent margin while live rules show 5 percent. The white paper on docs.chipforge.io still describes real-time winner-takes-all emissions with no probation, window or burn, and a 2025 roadmap (NPU by February 2026, tape-out files by November 2026); treat the live rules endpoint as current. The README's scoring section (function, area, performance and power weights) describes a generic mode; Quest 001's own package gives power no weight.
  • The record-ladder payout reached the public main branch on 2026-10-17, after Quest 001 closed, and the last Quest 001 winner record shows payment from the qualifying moment for 24 hours. Only that one record is visible on the team's endpoint; the other 20 were not checked, and code on a branch does not prove what validators ran.
  • The challenge server that sets challenges, batches, margins and winners is run by the team. The live rules list one expected validator, and the leaderboard's validator agreement figures come from the team's own API. A chain read on 2026-10-21 found five validators on five separate coldkeys all pointing at UID 0, Tempo (360), commit-reveal (on, one epoch) and the weight rate limit (100 blocks) were read from the chain on 2026-10-23 and match the team's snapshot.
  • Quest 001 scoring settings (gates, exponents, the 1 square millimetre area floor, the 62.89 MHz clock ceiling) come from the archived public package read on 2026-10-23; its checksums were not verified and the team server runs its own copy. Evaluator internals beyond the settings files, and an audit set of four smaller models named in the configuration, were not read.
  • Miners disputed the short reward window, the margin and the 90 percent burn during October, and the team kept the window for Quest 001.
  • Plans for open-source designs, fabricated chips and reinvested revenue are statements by the team; no revenue figures were found, and no evidence of tape-out preparation turned up in the chat, repository or API. The NPU phase, targeted for February 2026 in the white paper, reached a mainnet contest in October 2026. The technical architecture PDF in the repository was not read.
  • The public chipforge-challenges repository only holds one 2025 RISC-V challenge (last commit 2025-11-28); Quest 001 files are not there and come through the miner tool's download.
  • Earlier posts and the old repository name call this subnet SN84 and mention test network UID 440. The repository was renamed to SN108 on 2026-09-24 and the subnet was registered on mainnet on 2026-09-20 (block 9111253, read from the chain). The reason it is 108 rather than 84 was not found; netuid 84 is another project's slot.
