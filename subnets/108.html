What it is

ChipForge pays miners to design AI-accelerator chips in Verilog; validators score them with open tools. Run by the Tatsu team, it was subnet 84 until deregistered on 2026-05-01 and returned as subnet 108 on 2026-09-20. Its first contest here, Quest 001, closed on 2026-10-15.

How it works

Miners

Miners download a challenge package, design and test locally, then submit a signed zip of Verilog or SystemVerilog. Winning designs are shown to registered hotkeys 18 hours after the win, or sooner if overtaken.

Validators

Validators run the team's evaluation server. Since 2026-10-30 their software installs the signed evaluator itself and no longer picks winners: a team-run challenge server decides the records and sends the payout schedule they follow.

Incentive

Each new best design is a 'record': it must beat the previous best by a margin, wait out a probation, then is paid for a fixed window; up to three records share the pay. Only a tenth of validator weight is meant for miners; the rest goes to UID 0.

Roles and requirements

Miner Medium barrier

Designs the chip in Verilog or SystemVerilog and submits it for scoring.

  • Live rules (2026-10-31): 1 submission per hotkey, 60 minutes apart; Quest 001 allowed 5.
  • Quest 001's score to beat rose from under 50 to about 2,294 in nine days: expect competition.

Validator High barrier

Runs the team's evaluation server, scores designs and sets weights.

  • Five hold permits and all set weights within the last tempo (2026-10-31); UID 0 holds about 61% of validator dividends.
  • One design at a time, about 3 hours each on the minimum machine (the guide also says 1.5 to 2 hours).

Getting started

  1. Register a hotkey on subnet 108 (0.05 TAO on 2026-10-31) and clone ChipForge_SN108; the README covers installation.
  2. Check the team's public status page for an active challenge; none is open on 2026-10-31.
  3. When one is open, download its package with the miner tool, design in Verilog, score locally and submit the signed zip (up to 50 MB).
  4. For Quest 2, plan a 16-core, 32 GiB RAM, 300 GiB disk test machine (EDA server guide).

Caveats

  • Nothing is paid between contests; the Quest 2 date is a team estimate and its specification is not public.
  • UID 0 is the owner's own hotkey; what its validator dividends (about 61%) do was not traced.
  • Winners, payouts and leaderboard figures come from the team's own server.
  • Open-source designs, fabricated chips and reinvested revenue are team statements; no revenue, customers or tape-out preparation were found.
