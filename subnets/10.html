What it is

Pareton (SN10) pays miners to speed up open-source AI model serving. The team pins a model, GPU setup and engine; miners submit code patches, benchmarked in rounds, and the best verified patch leads until beaten by 1 percent. The team wants paying customers; none are shown.

How it works

Miners

Write a git patch against the campaign's pinned commit, upload it and record its hash on chain with the repo's CLI, which pays the fee. Patches stay private unless a campaign opts into a delayed reveal.

Validators

The team's watcher checks each patch, builds it in a pinned container and benchmarks it against the leader on one rented pod, then checks answer quality. Score: how much sooner each prompt-length tier finishes than on the baseline, minus a penalty for failed prompts. A challenger must beat the leader by 1 percent. Other validators copy the team's weights.

Incentive

Emission splits 18 percent owner, 41 validators, 41 miners. About 54 percent of the miner share is aimed at the owner's burn UID and destroyed. The leader's weight falls in a straight line from the campaign's share to 0 over about 28 days; today it is about 46 percent of the miner share, roughly 19 percent of all emission. With no leader, everything burns.

Roles and requirements

Miner High barrier

Writes a patch that speeds up the campaign's pinned engine and commits it on chain with a fee.

  • Only the public baseline image counts; pull it by digest, since a local rebuild scores differently.
  • A patch is one git diff against the pinned commit, allowed paths only. Use --dry-run; avoid --timelock, which never reveals. Duplicates are charged and dropped.
  • Only the leader earns, from about 72 minutes after taking the seat.

Validator Medium barrier

Sets weights: copies the team's vector with a script, or runs its own full benchmark stack.

  • The team runs the validator that decides leaders (UID 201, the owner's UID).
  • On 2026-10-28, 16 hotkeys held permits; 14 had set weights within 72 minutes, all with top weight on UID 201.
  • The copy script submits the team's vector every 360 blocks. Own stack: add swap on a 16 GB host, since out-of-memory kills wrongly reject patches.

Bull case

  • Team ships often and answers miners
  • Open code and API; a clear winner-takes-all rule
  • A Muse Spark campaign is being prepared

Bear case

  • Team sets pay; over half of the miner share burns
  • No paying customer shown
  • Only campaign is closing; leader pay decays to 0

What would change this view: A successor campaign with a stated reward, a named paying customer or steady inflow would improve it; a closed campaign with no successor would worsen it.

Getting started

  1. Check the dashboard for an open campaign; the only one is winding down, so watch the channel.
  2. Read the campaign manifest: pinned commit, allowed paths, model, GPU, scoring rule, fee.
  3. Pull the public baseline image and benchmark your patch on matching GPUs.
  4. Run the miner CLI with --dry-run, then submit.
  5. Expect delay: builds take hours and a round needs a free pod.

Caveats

  • The team sets pay: validators submit its published vector, and 14 put their top weight on the owner's UID. Docs say commit-reveal is on; the chain setting is off (2026-10-28).
  • Docs lag the code: they describe a median-speedup score and one validator.
  • Round timing is uneven; an unmerged branch adds a switch that pauses round creation, and any pause is not visible.
  • Partner and speedup claims (Engy, 20 to 54 percent, a Leadpoet pilot, a tokenised SN10 on Base) are unverified.
  • The slot dates from 2024-05-02; an earlier project, Swap, held it before the team claimed it in July 2026.
