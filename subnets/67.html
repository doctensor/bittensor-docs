What it is

Harnyx is a contest for deep-research agents. Miners submit one Python file that answers research-style questions with cited sources under a per-task cost and time budget. Validators run each agent in a sandbox and a judge compares its answers with stronger reference answers. The team's stated aim is deep research sold as an API, "deep research as a commodity", and its website offers a Research API in experimental alpha behind a waitlist. The subnet is a harness competition: the repo's thesis is that better workflows around a model matter as much as the model. The team's launch post is dated 2026-03-26, and the chain shows emission starting at about that time.

How it works

Miners

A miner writes agent.py (one UTF-8 file, at most 1 MB) with a query entrypoint, using the harnyx-miner-sdk. The agent gets a query and returns text or a structured output plus numbered citations, and may add an optional note. Inside the sandbox it calls platform tools: llm_chat (providers Chutes, OpenRouter, AI Gateway and SayGM), search_web and fetch_page (Desearch, Parallel, Firecrawl, Exa, Tavily, DeepSeek, Expand), embeddings and a decision_query tool for small yes/no or scoring calls. Since 2026-10-21 any paid OpenRouter model may be used; free models are excluded. Miners store their own provider API keys on the platform with harnyx-miner-config; the platform uses them through a proxy during evaluation, so the miner pays the provider bills, and each selected validator runs the agent separately for every task. Each task has a spend budget (the default in code is 0.50 USD, and the docs say it can vary), counted at regular pre-discount prices, and a time limit. Since 2026-10-20 the limit is set per task from how two reference solvers did: 300 seconds if neither solved it, 200 if one did, 100 if both did, and 70 percent of that for fast-mode tasks. Since 2026-10-22 the repo treats an OpenRouter call that failed without a known reference price as a recoverable tool error the agent can catch, retry or route around, instead of failing the whole task; the team has not said when that went live. Miners test locally with harnyx-miner-local-eval against a completed batch (and harnyx-miner-local-benchmark against public benchmark suites), then sign and upload with harnyx-miner-submit. A newly submitted agent first goes through a duplicate check against recent champions and recent batches. When an artifact becomes champion, the platform also runs public benchmark suites on it using the miner's stored keys.

Validators

The platform is run by the Harnyx team and is not in the public repo. It builds the tasks and reference answers, keeps the work ledger, aggregates results, picks the champion and records the emission allocation. Validators are Docker services (2 to 4 vCPU, 32 GiB RAM, a public endpoint on port 8100, a Chutes key) that poll the platform for assignments, run agent-by-task in sandboxes with 4 concurrent artifact sandboxes, and score each answer with a pairwise LLM judge against the reference, in both orders. A task score is the share of the two comparisons the miner wins. Validators also vote on duplicate and novelty labels, and fetch the platform's allocation from a signed GET /v1/weights call to set weights on chain. One successful validator delivery is enough for a batch to count, and batch work goes only to validators on a healthy, allowlisted list. The team says validator code changes about daily, and on 2026-10-22 it promised to announce changes that affect miners hours before a batch. On 2026-10-23 the monitoring API listed 9 validator endpoints: 4 healthy, 3 unhealthy and 2 unknown, and 4 ran the newest version (20261022.post5). On chain, 8 UIDs held validator permits and all 8 had set weights within the previous 250 blocks. Their weight rows matched one platform allocation, as the validator README describes (46 targets each at 04:30 UTC; at 15:00 UTC the two rows read had 131 and 129 targets with the same shares). The subnet owner's hotkey is one of the 8 and held about 73 to 76 percent of the validator dividends in the two reads.

Incentive

One batch runs per day: submissions up to 15:00 UTC are included, the batch runs overnight and in the last ten days completed between about 02:00 and 07:00 UTC the next day. Every non-duplicate submission does 10 qualifying tasks. Across validators, each task result is the median of the validators' scores, an artifact's score is the sum of those medians, and its cost and runtime are medians of each validator's total at regular prices. Each challenger is compared with the sitting champion, and the champion plus up to 29 ranked challengers that pass a rule against it go to a main round with 20 more tasks. The champion is then chosen by a cascade, not by highest score: starting from the incumbent and taking challengers in submission order, a challenger replaces the incumbent only if it beats it by at least 10 percent of the maximum score, or matches its score and runtime with median cost at least 10 percent lower, or matches its score and cost with median runtime at least 10 percent and 1,000 ms lower. If the incumbent scores zero in the round, the first challenger with a positive score takes over. In the seven completed batches with cutoffs 2026-10-16 to 10-22 the cascade had 11 steps where a challenger took the title: 5 on cost, 2 on runtime, 3 on score margin and 1 on the positive-score rule. In 12 of the 19 completed batches since 2026-10-03 the highest raw scorer was not the final champion. Because challengers are taken in order, earlier submissions have an advantage, and the team has said so. Emission has two parts. A newly chosen champion artifact can earn up to 50 percent of miner emission, 25 percent if the same artifact holds on in the next successful batch and 12.5 percent after that, scaled by its batch score: on 2026-10-23 the champion's score of 0.153 gave 7.6 percent. The rest is split among reward-eligible artifacts by participation stage (top 50 percent 1, top 10 percent 2, main round 5 times) and a novelty label (near duplicate 1, notable change 3, novel 10 times), then summed per hotkey. Duplicates, zero-response artifacts and an unchanged incumbent get no participation share. Shares for hotkeys that are no longer registered are not paid and, per the docs, burn through the owner UID: at 04:03 UTC on 2026-10-23 that was 21 of 260 participating hotkeys and 9.4 percent of miner emission, with 117 hotkeys paid and 122 getting nothing; by about 15:00 UTC, for the same batch, the API showed 7 unregistered hotkeys, 2.5 percent burned and 131 paid recipients, because miners had registered in between. In a failed batch, all miner emission is split equally among distinct participating hotkeys. What the chain shows. The subnet was registered on 2026-01-03, has 256 of 256 UID slots, a 360-block tempo (72 minutes), commit-reveal weights with a one-epoch reveal period and one mechanism; the chain's emission split is 18 percent owner, 41 validator and 41 miner, and unused emission is burned rather than recycled. At blocks 9346946 to 9347024 (about 15:00 to 15:10 UTC on 2026-10-23) 131 UIDs had non-zero incentive, the top UID held 9.7 percent (the batch champion's hotkey, which sits on UID 254, not on UID 111 as the batch post says), the next 6.9 percent, many sat at exactly 2.1 percent, and the owner UID 0 had 2.6 percent. The two validator weight rows read at that block (UIDs 11 and 220, after the commits had been revealed) matched the API's allocation per UID to a tenth of a percent, except for the owner UID and one to three small UIDs missing from a row. That also fits the docs' burn route: the API's owner share of 2.5 percent shows up as the owner UID's incentive. Eight validator permits exist (UIDs 0, 5, 7, 11, 12, 128, 220, 246); all had set weights within 261 blocks. The owner's UID 0 is one of them and held about 76 percent of validator dividends, 39 percent of last epoch's alpha emission; UIDs 11 and 220 held 12 and 6 percent. Allocations are read per hotkey, so the UID in a batch post can differ from the UID paid later. At 04:30 UTC the chain still showed the previous allocation (46 non-zero UIDs) while the API already showed the new champion, because revealed weights lag the batch by about an hour or two.

Getting started

  1. Read the Mining Runbook and miner README in the repo, then clone it and run uv sync --all-packages --dev.
  2. Have a hotkey registered on subnet 67 (shares for unregistered hotkeys are not paid) and create a .env with PLATFORM_BASE_URL=https://api.harnyx.ai plus a Chutes key and a search provider key for local evaluation.
  3. Connect an MCP client to https://api.harnyx.ai/mcp and call get_champion and get_miner_script to read the current champion as a baseline.
  4. Run harnyx-miner-local-eval --agent-path ./agent.py, optionally with --batch-id of a completed batch, and fix the repeated failures in its report. harnyx-miner-local-benchmark --list-suites shows the public benchmark suites you can also test against, and the repo's miner/skills folder and AUTO-RESEARCH.md describe a step-by-step and an autonomous improvement loop.
  5. Store the provider keys your agent needs with harnyx-miner-config (and set a task retry count of 0 to 3 if wanted) before the batch is created.
  6. Hash and upload with harnyx-miner-hash and harnyx-miner-submit, then confirm the artifact appears in get_latest_submissions. Anything submitted after 15:00 UTC waits for the next daily batch.
  7. After the batch completes, use the MCP comparison and result tools to see scores, costs, errors and the champion steps.
  8. Validators: follow validator/README.md (copy .env.example, set the platform URL, public URL and Chutes key, run scripts/operator_up.sh).

Caveats

  • Chain data were read twice on 2026-10-23: blocks 9343853 to 9343915 (about 04:30 to 04:42 UTC) and blocks 9346946 to 9347024 (about 15:00 to 15:10 UTC), with addresses masked. Weights are validator opinions, not payouts; with commit-reveal the rows are what was revealed, so the chain trails the API by an hour or two. The API's champion hotkey was on UID 111 in the batch posts and on UID 254 at the 15:00 read, and the allocation is paid per hotkey to whatever UID the hotkey holds; UIDs in posts and in the API record can be stale. The chain match was checked for two validators' rows, not all eight, and the similar 8-row shape at 04:30 UTC was a different allocation. Owner-share figures depend on the owner hotkey test (hotkey and coldkey equal the subnet owner), which cannot see other coldkeys the team may hold.
  • The platform (task generation, aggregation, champion choice, allocation) is run by the team and its code is not public. Validators fetch the allocation from it, and batch work goes only to allowlisted validators. Scoring and task quality cannot be independently reproduced from the public repo.
  • The task regime changed on 2026-10-05, 2026-10-15 and 2026-10-20, and the validator code changes about daily. Scores from before 2026-10-15 are not comparable with later ones: the best main-round score fell from about 0.72 to between 0.15 and 0.32. The champion changed in 18 of the 19 completed batches since 2026-10-03, usually on small score gaps decided by cost or runtime, so the title is unstable at today's score levels.
  • The 2026-10-22 incident numbers (135 of 251 scored artifacts with a zero qualifying total) come from the team's monitoring API. Miners quoted 209 of 251 scripts hit and a billing-check cause; the team acknowledged a mid-batch validator rollout but this page did not verify the cause or the 209 figure, and the repo fixes dated 10-22 are not confirmed as deployed.
  • Main-round size varies between batches in the API (5 to 30 artifacts since 2026-10-03). A user asked about this on 2026-10-20 and no answer was seen. It fits the code, where only challengers that pass a rule against the incumbent in qualifying are admitted, but the team has not confirmed that explanation.
  • The API's batch summary calls the similarity stage outcome 'kept incumbent' for every completed batch from 2026-10-03 to 10-22, including 18 where the final decision was a champion change. Its field names (champion after the similarity stage) suggest it describes the step before the main-round cascade. The repo docs do not explain it and the team has not confirmed this reading.
  • A 2026-09-09 team post said any invalid citation fails the response on the default path. The current judge prompt in the repo (2026-10-22) treats a bad citation pointer as a quality defect, never an automatic failure. The page follows the current code; the history in between was not checked.
  • The causes of the 2026-10-16 failed batch and of the failed DRACO benchmark gradings (all 20 items on the runs finished 10-21, 10-22 and 10-23) were not published.
  • Public benchmark samples (20 items per suite) are not comparable with batch scores: the batch tasks are newly generated questions that frontier models missed, so batch scores of 0.15 to 0.23 sit beside benchmark results of 0.5 to 0.8.
  • Black-box mining (miners registering a public HTTPS endpoint) is described in the repo as not yet released, with its emission at zero. The monitoring API showed a zero black-box share on 2026-10-23.
  • Miners pay the model and search bills for every task, for every validator that runs their agent. No cost figures are given here, and provider and model lists change often; use tooling_info() in an agent for the live list.
  • The channel's automated assistant (Nabu) is run by the team and answers many questions, posts batch summaries and a summary of repo changes, but its answers are not formal policy, and many of its batch summaries say the analysis could not be completed. Its commentary on token price was not used.
  • Reward rules depend on a batch data version: version 9 and later uses the 50, 25, 12.5 percent champion cap and 10 times novelty; older batches replay older rules.
  • The dashboard is client-rendered, so figures were read from the public monitoring API on 2026-10-23 and will have moved.
